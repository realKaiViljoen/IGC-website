# IGC — UK Multi-Channel Outreach Playbook
**Insight Growth Consulting | Market: United Kingdom | Niche: Independent IT MSPs**
**Compiled: April 2026 | Status: Authoritative Operational Reference**

> This document is the master outreach rulebook. It governs which channel, what message, when, and what to do next for every possible response — from first touch to closed deal or confirmed disqualification. Every rule in this document is a hard rule. "Guideline" appears nowhere. If you are uncertain what to do next for any prospect at any stage, the answer is in this document.

---

## PART 1: CHANNEL HIERARCHY AND RATIONALE

### The Four Channels (in Order)

**Channel 1: LinkedIn Connection Request**
**Channel 2: Cold Call**
**Channel 3: Email**
**Channel 4: LinkedIn DM Re-engagement**

---

### Channel 1: LinkedIn — Why First

LinkedIn is the operating environment of the ICP. IT MSP owners are on LinkedIn regularly — less so for candidate sourcing, but more for industry networking, Microsoft partner events, vendor announcements, service launches, and peer conversations with other MSP operators. A connection request is native to their workflow. It costs them nothing to accept and nothing to ignore.

The strategic function of the connection request is not to start a conversation. It is to establish presence. When the cold call arrives on Day 5, they have seen the name. When the email arrives on Day 10, they recognise the brand. The connection request is the reconnaissance move that makes every subsequent channel warmer.

A personalised connection request (250–300 chars) with a specific hook — a job posting they made, a content post they wrote, a shared contact — achieves an acceptance rate of 28–38% with ICP-matched prospects. This is the highest-leverage entry point.

**Why LinkedIn before cold call:**
- Zero cost of rejection (they simply decline or ignore — no hostile interaction)
- Warms the prospect before first voice contact
- Provides a reason-to-call ("I sent you a connection request and then thought it was worth picking up the phone")
- Establishes IGC as a professional peer, not a cold number on a screen

---

### Channel 2: Cold Call — Why Second, Not First

The cold call is the highest-conversion single touch when landed correctly, but it requires context to survive the first 10 seconds. Calling with zero prior touchpoint means the prospect has no frame for who you are. The LinkedIn connection — even if not accepted — gives you a reference point: "I reached out on LinkedIn earlier this week."

Cold calls for UK independent MSP owners work in the 10:00–12:00 and 16:00–17:30 GMT windows on Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday. These are not soft guidelines — they are the windows where owner-operators are accessible and not in the middle of a client escalation or on a service-desk firefight.

**Why cold call is Channel 2 and not Channel 3:**
- Voice contact establishes credibility that text cannot replicate at this price point
- A 90-second call answered and handled well achieves more than 5 follow-up emails
- The phone number from Apollo/Cognism is a perishable asset — use it while it's valid
- Email has a 2–4% response rate for cold B2B outreach. Cold calls to qualified UK IT MSP owner lists hit 8–14% positive engagement when the script is tight and the caller has peer-level credibility in the MSP space

**Rule:** Maximum 3 dial attempts per prospect in the calling phase (Days 5, 8, 12). If not connected after 3 attempts with voicemails left: move to email.

---

### Channel 3: Email — Why Third

Email is the follow-through instrument, not the opening move. It serves two functions: (1) following up on a call attempt that produced a voicemail, and (2) delivering a value asset (case study, one-pager) that creates a reason to respond without requiring an immediate conversation.

Email open rates for cold B2B to UK SMEs run 18–28%. Reply rates to cold outreach run 2–5%. These numbers do not justify leading with email. However, email excels at staying in the field of vision of an MSP owner who is not yet ready to engage — which is the majority of the pipeline at any given time.

**Why email is Channel 3:**
- Supplements calls already made (prospect has heard the name twice before this lands)
- Allows delivery of assets that cannot be conveyed in 90 seconds on the phone
- Creates a written record the prospect can refer to when timing is right
- Easier to track (opens, clicks) and triggers re-engagement signals

**Hard rule:** Never send an email before attempting the LinkedIn connection. Every email should reference a prior touchpoint.

---

### Channel 4: LinkedIn DM Re-engagement — Why Fourth

LinkedIn DMs to connected contacts have a dramatically higher open rate than cold email (typically 30–50% for ICP-matched 1st-degree connections). This channel is reserved for prospects who have already accepted the connection but have not responded to earlier DMs or calls. It is a re-engagement instrument, not an opening one.

The DM at this stage serves as a low-friction final check-in before the prospect enters the nurture sequence. It is personal, conversational, and asks a single yes/no question.

---

### When Channel Order Changes

**EXCEPTION 1: You have a verified mobile number with no LinkedIn profile (rare, but possible from Cognism).**
- Channel 1 becomes: Cold call first, then LinkedIn search and connection request.
- Rationale: If you have the phone number but no LinkedIn connection lever, the call creates the initial touchpoint rather than warming a future one.

**EXCEPTION 2: Prospect is already a 1st-degree connection before outreach begins.**
- Channel 1 becomes: LinkedIn DM directly (skip connection request).
- Channel 2 becomes: Cold call if DM produces no response after 4 days.
- Channel 3 remains: Email.
- Rationale: The connection lever is already exercised. Go straight to the message.

**EXCEPTION 3: Prospect has engaged with your LinkedIn content (liked, commented) in the past 30 days.**
- Channel 1 becomes: LinkedIn DM referencing their engagement.
- This is now a warm outreach, not cold. The call and email sequence is secondary.
- Rationale: They already signalled awareness. Strike while the signal is hot.

**EXCEPTION 4: Prospect is a Tier 1 (4+ signals, job posting for engineer/account manager live this week).**
- Run Channel 1 (LinkedIn) and Channel 2 (call attempt) on the same day — morning LinkedIn, afternoon call.
- Rationale: Tier 1 signal clusters indicate active pain right now. Speed matters more than sequencing elegance.

## PART 2: THE COMPLETE 90-DAY OUTREACH CALENDAR

### Definitions
- **BST** = British Summer Time (GMT+1, applies April–October). Use GMT in winter.
- **Connected** = LinkedIn connection accepted.
- **Not connected** = Connection pending or declined.
- **Active** = Prospect is in pursuit phase, not yet disqualified or moved to nurture.
- **Attempt** = A dial, regardless of outcome (answer, voicemail, no answer, engaged).

### DAY 0 — ENTRY

**Channel:** LinkedIn  
**Action:** Send connection request  
**Content:** Variant based on signal available:

*Variant A — Job posting hook (use if they have an active "Engineer" or "Account Manager" posting):*
> "Hi [Name] — saw [Company] is hiring a [engineer/account manager]. Timing's interesting — I work with independent IT MSPs on exactly the BD infrastructure question that comes up when you're growing your team. Worth connecting."

*Variant B — No job posting, generic ICP signal:*
> "Hi [Name] — I work specifically with independent IT MSPs on client acquisition infrastructure. We don't run BD for you — we build the system so you're not the only one generating new contracts. Worth a connection."

*Variant C — Shared network or content engagement:*
> "Hi [Name] — saw your post on [topic] / we share [mutual name]. I work with independent IT MSPs on new business pipeline infrastructure — seems relevant to where you're operating. Happy to connect."

**Expected outcome:** 28–38% acceptance rate within 5 days. No follow-up required on Day 0.  
**HubSpot:** Stage = `Not Contacted` → update to `Connection Requested`. Log: connection note sent, variant used, date.  
**Task created:** "Check LinkedIn connection status" — due Day 3.

## PART 3: DECISION TREE — ALL BRANCHES

### How to Use This Tree

Every branch begins with an observed event. Find the event that matches your situation. Follow the exact next action and timing specified. Do not improvise.

---

### BRANCH 1: They Accepted LinkedIn + Responded Positively to Step 1 DM

**Trigger:** They reply to the Step 1 DM with anything other than "not interested" or silence. Even a "tell me more" or "what does it cost?" counts as positive.

**Immediate action (within 4 business hours of seeing the reply):**

Reply to the DM. Do not go straight to a pitch. Respond to what they actually said.

*If they asked what it costs:*
> "Happy to walk you through the economics. The short version: £3,500 build, £1,750/month retainer (entirely optional after the build). One new 30-seat managed service contract at typical MSP rates covers the build multiple times over.
>
> Rather than go back and forth in DMs — are you open to a 20-minute call? I can show you exactly what gets built and answer the cost question properly. [Calendly link]"

*If they said "tell me more" or "sounds interesting":*
> "A few questions so I don't waste your time:
>
> 1. Is your new business mainly coming from referrals at the moment, or do you have proactive outreach running?
> 2. Is the constraint new contracts, contract renewals, or something else?
> 3. What's your current outreach approach — LinkedIn, referrals, events, something else?
>
> Happy to do this in a quick call if easier. [Calendly link]"

**HubSpot:** Stage = `Replied`. Task: "Follow up if no booking in 24h" — due tomorrow.

**If they reply to your qualification questions:**  
- If answers confirm ICP fit (referral-dependent, new contract constraint, 5–50 headcount): Book the discovery call.
- If answers reveal disqualifying information (they have a dedicated BD hire, they're primarily a break-fix shop, headcount > 50): Go to Walk-Away Rules (Part 6) — soft disqualify.

**If they don't reply to your qualification questions within 3 business days:**  
Send one follow-up DM:
> "[Name] — just checking if you saw my questions below. Happy to answer anything about the system first if that's more useful."

If still no reply after 2 more days: they have de-prioritised this. Move to email channel. Send Email 1 referencing the DM exchange.

---

### BRANCH 2: They Accepted LinkedIn + No Response to Step 1 DM After 4 Days

**Trigger:** Connected, Step 1 DM sent, 4 days elapsed, no reply.

**Action:** Proceed to Day 10 Step 2 DM (as per 90-day calendar). Do not send a follow-up DM before Day 10. The 4-day gap is not long enough to follow up — it reads as desperate.

**No exceptions to the 4-day minimum before Step 2 DM.**

---

### BRANCH 3: They Rejected LinkedIn Connection (Declined)

**Trigger:** LinkedIn shows connection as declined (rare — most non-responses are simply pending).

**Action:**
1. Do not try to re-send a LinkedIn connection request. LinkedIn will flag it.
2. Move immediately to cold call if you have a verified phone number.
3. If no phone number: move to email only.
4. Cold call opener: "Hi [Name], I tried to connect with you on LinkedIn last week — I think you declined, which is completely fair. I'm calling because I work with independent IT MSPs on client acquisition and I wanted to make sure I wasn't missing a relevant conversation by hiding behind LinkedIn."
5. Email subject (if no phone): `Couldn't reach you on LinkedIn — quick question`

**HubSpot:** Note the declined connection. Do not update stage — continue outreach through remaining channels.

---

### BRANCH 4: Cold Call Answered + Interested

**Trigger:** Prospect answers the call and responds positively (doesn't hang up, asks a question, says "go on", asks about cost).

**On the call — immediate priority actions:**

1. Complete the 30-second pitch if they gave permission.
2. Answer their questions directly. Do not hedge.
3. Qualify before booking: "Before I take any more of your time — are you the decision-maker on something like this, or would this involve your business partner / a second director?" (If yes: proceed. If no: "Who should I be speaking with? Happy to include them on a call.")
4. Book the discovery call before ending the call. Do not let them say "send me some info and I'll get back to you" without first attempting to secure a date: "I've got slots Tuesday at 11am and Thursday at 3pm BST — either of those work? Or I can send you a Calendly link. The call is 30 minutes, I'll walk you through exactly what gets built and you can tell me if it's relevant."

**If they say "send me more info first":**
> "Happy to. I'll send you a one-pager with the full model, ROI breakdown, and a case study. Quick question before I do — roughly when would be a good time for a follow-up call if the info looks relevant? Next week?"

**Post-call action (within 30 minutes):**
- Send the "Interested prospect" email (below) with one-pager attached.
- Create follow-up call task for 3 days after info sent.

*Post-call email subject:* `[Company] — client acquisition system overview`

*Body:*
> [Name],
>
> Great speaking with you just now.
>
> As promised — attached is the one-pager covering exactly what gets built, the 30-day timeline, pricing, and the guarantee mechanics.
>
> [Calendly link] if you want to book directly. I'll also follow up [date] if I haven't heard back.
>
> [Your name]
> IGC

**HubSpot:** Stage = `Replied`. If call booked: Stage = `Call Booked`. Note full call summary (their situation, key signals, objections raised).

---

### BRANCH 5: Cold Call Answered + Not Interested (Soft No)

**Trigger:** They answer, hear the pitch, and say something like "not for us", "we're fine thanks", "we use referrals", "we have someone for that."

**On the call — do not accept the soft no immediately. Use one turn:**

> "Can I ask — is that because the timing isn't right, or because you've genuinely solved the new business pipeline problem already? I ask because if it's timing I won't keep calling, but I'll touch base in a few months. If you've genuinely cracked it, I'd love to understand how."

**If they clarify it's solved:** "Appreciate you being straight. I won't bother you again. Good luck with it." — End call professionally. HubSpot: Note their stated reason. Stage = `Nurture` with note "Says BD solved — low priority."

**If they clarify it's timing:** "That makes sense. When would be a sensible time for me to reach out — is Q3 realistic, or is it more of a next year conversation?" Take their answer literally. Create a task for contact at their stated time. HubSpot: Stage = `Nurture`. Tag: `Re-engage: [month/quarter they stated]`.

**If they become vague or brush off:** Thank them, end the call, and move to the quarterly nurture sequence. Do not call again within 90 days.

**Post-call action:** No email immediately. Let 48 hours pass. If they said to come back in a specific time: set the task. If vague: move to nurture, Day 90 email is next touch.

---

### BRANCH 6: Cold Call Answered + Hard No

**Trigger:** Clear, hostile, or definitive: "Don't call me again", "Take me off your list", "We're not interested — don't contact us", "How did you get this number?"

**On the call:**
> "Understood. I'll remove you from our list entirely. Apologies for the intrusion." — Hang up immediately. No pitch. No one more turn.

**Post-call action (within 10 minutes):**
- HubSpot: `igc_outreach_stage` = `disqualified`. Reason = "Hard no — requested removal."
- Add to HubSpot suppression list (do not contact).
- If they said "don't contact us again" — this applies to ALL channels (LinkedIn DMs, email).
- Remove LinkedIn connection if connected.
- Set contact status = `Disqualified`. Deal stage = `Closed Lost`.

**Hard rule:** A hard no is permanent. Do not re-approach for minimum 12 months, and only if: (a) you have a materially different offer, and (b) a different person at the company initiates contact with you.

---

### BRANCH 7: Cold Call Voicemail Left + No Callback in 48 Hours

**Trigger:** Left voicemail on Day 5 or Day 8, 48 hours have elapsed, no callback.

**Action:** Do not call again immediately. Send Email 1 (see Day 7 of 90-day calendar). The voicemail reference in the email provides social context and continuity. Continue the outreach sequence as planned. The voicemail's job is to prime the email, not to generate a callback.

**Hard rule:** Never leave more than 2 voicemails across the entire active pursuit phase. One on Day 5, one on Day 8. After 2 voicemails with no callback: calls continue (Day 12) but no further voicemails.

---

### BRANCH 8: Cold Call No Answer — 3 Attempts, No Voicemail Callback

**Trigger:** Called Day 5, Day 8, Day 12. No answer on any attempt, or voicemails left with no callback.

**Action:**
- No further outbound calls during this sequence.
- Phone channel is exhausted.
- Continue email sequence (Email 2 on Day 14, Email 3 on Day 21).
- Continue LinkedIn DMs if connected (Steps 2 and 3 as per calendar).

**HubSpot:** `igc_outreach_stage` = `called_no_answer_3x`. Note the 3 attempt dates and windows used.

**If phone is clearly invalid (immediate error tone, disconnected message):** Skip all remaining call attempts. Update `igc_phone_verified` = `unverified`. Email-only track from this point.

---

### BRANCH 9: Email Opened, No Reply

**Trigger:** HubSpot tracking shows email was opened (1 or more times) but no reply received.

**What an open tells you:** They saw it. The subject line worked. The timing was not right to respond or the friction to reply felt too high.

**Action:**
- Do not send a follow-up email immediately saying "just checking you saw this" — it is condescending and they clearly saw it.
- Continue the sequence as planned. Send the next scheduled email on its scheduled day.
- If an email has been opened 3+ times with no reply, that is a buying signal. Move the call task to the top of the queue and call earlier if the window allows.
- One specific exception: if Email 2 (the ROI email) is opened 2+ times, send a personalised LinkedIn DM within 24 hours:

*DM (Email 2 opened 2+ times):*

## PART 4: SPECIAL OFFER RULES

### The Floor

**Build fee floor: £3,000.** Below this number, the economics do not work for IGC (tool costs + IGC labour for a 30-day build). Never go below £3,000. If a prospect's absolute ceiling is below £3,000, the engagement is not viable. Walk away.

**Retainer floor: £1,500/month.** Below this, the ongoing work is not commercially viable. If they push the retainer below £1,500: let the retainer go, handover cleanly, and let them run it themselves.

---

### TRIGGER 1: They express genuine interest but have a verifiable cash flow constraint

This is not "I want a discount" — it is "I want to proceed but the outlay timing is genuinely difficult." You will know this because:
- They've confirmed they want to proceed.
- They ask about payment timing (not price reduction).
- They reference a specific cash flow event (slow month, delayed invoice, large hardware outlay they just made).

**The special: Payment split.**
- £1,750 on agreement signing.
- £1,750 at Day 15 (when the system goes live).
- Total: £3,500 — unchanged.
- Framing: "I don't normally split the build fee, but I can make an exception here — £1,750 now and £1,750 when the system is live on Day 15. The total is the same; it's just spread across the build."

---

### TRIGGER 2: They are Tier 1 (4+ signals, high-intent), verbally confirmed interest on a call, and the only stated obstacle is the build fee

This is a genuine near-close where price is the single blocking variable, not a general objection.

**The special: Guarantee extension.**
- Standard guarantee: 5 qualified prospect conversations in 30 days or free extension.
- Enhanced guarantee: 5 qualified prospect conversations in 30 days or we extend AND give you the first month retainer free.
- This adds £1,750 of value without reducing the build fee.
- Framing: "I'm confident this will work for [Company] based on what you've told me. I'll extend the guarantee: 5 conversations in 30 days, or I keep running it for free AND your first retainer month is at no charge. That means the maximum you're exposed to is the £3,500 build fee — and even that is covered by one managed service contract."

---

### TRIGGER 3: End of month / end of quarter with open build capacity

**Hard rule:** If it is the last 5 business days of the month AND you have a build slot that will otherwise go unfilled AND a prospect is genuinely interested but stuck on timing:

- Trigger the "last slot" closing frame.
- This is not a discount — it is a scarcity signal that is true: IGC builds a maximum of 6 MSPs per quarter. If the slot goes unfilled, it is genuinely gone.
- Framing: "I want to be transparent — I have one build slot left this month, and I'm speaking with two other MSPs. I'm not trying to pressure you, but I'd rather give you the first option than have you reach out next month and find I'm full. If you're serious about moving forward, now is the time."

**This frame is only deployed when both conditions are true:**
1. The slot is genuinely limited (you are genuinely near capacity).
2. The prospect has expressed genuine interest previously (not a cold first touch).

Never deploy this as a manipulation tactic on a cold prospect who has expressed no interest. That destroys credibility.

---

### The Special Offer Rule: When to Deploy

**Rule:** Never offer a special on the first price objection. The sequence is:

1. First objection: Reframe the economics (ROI, one-contract cover, guaranteed outcome).
2. Second objection (they come back and say price is still the issue): Ask whether it's cash flow or total cost. If cash flow: offer the payment split. If total cost: go to enhanced guarantee.
3. Only if they have come back twice with price as the objection and you have confirmed they are otherwise committed: offer the relevant special based on the trigger above.

A prospect who objects to price once and drops it is not a candidate for a special — they were testing. A prospect who comes back twice is genuinely constrained.

---

### Reading "Not Now" vs "Never Coming Back"

**Signals that "not yet" is genuine:**
- They name a specific event or date.
- They ask you to follow up at a specific time.
- They engage with your nurture emails (open rates, replies).
- They post about BD problems, contract churn, or new business challenges on LinkedIn (they are living the pain).
- Their company is growing (new job postings for engineers/account managers, headcount increase in Apollo).

**Signals that "not yet" means never:**
- Vague, uncommitted when pressed for timing.
- Don't engage with any nurture touch over 6 months.
- They've hired a dedicated sales director or BD person (solved the problem another way).
- They moved to a different company.
- Their company has shrunk below 5 people (no longer ICP — survival mode).
- They were hostile, even mildly, on the first interaction.

## PART 5: NURTURE VS. ACTIVE PURSUIT RULES

### Hard Rules — When to Move from Active to Nurture

**Rule 1:** After 8 touches with zero engagement (no reply, no open, no callback, no LinkedIn interaction) across a minimum of 21 days: move to nurture automatically. Do not make additional active pursuit attempts.

**Rule 2:** After a soft no on a cold call ("not right now" / "not for us") with no further qualifier: move to nurture. Active pursuit restarts only on a re-engagement signal.

**Rule 3:** After a proposal is sent and 10+ business days pass with no response despite 2 follow-up attempts: move to nurture. This is not disqualification — it is a timing issue. But continued active chasing damages the relationship.

**Rule 4:** If they explicitly say "come back in [month/quarter]": move to nurture with a task set for that exact time. Do not contact before that time regardless of other triggers.

---

### Soft Rules — "In 3 Months" Scenarios

When a prospect says "contact me in 3 months" or equivalent:

1. Create a task in HubSpot: `Re-engage: [specific month]`.
2. Send one confirmation: "I'll be in touch in [month]. In the meantime, I may send you one piece of market data I think you'll find useful — no pitch."
3. Send one nurture value email at the 45-day mark. This is not re-pitching — it is staying warm.
4. Re-engage with a call on the date they specified. Treat it as a warm call: "We spoke [date] and you asked me to come back in [month] — I'm calling as promised."
5. If they are not ready at their stated time: ask for a new window. One more 60-day extension is acceptable. After that: move to quarterly nurture.

---

### The Nurture Sequence — What It Contains

**Cadence:** 1 touch per 90 days (quarterly).

**Content rotation — never repeat content within 12 months:**

*Touch 1 (Day 90):* Market data. A specific number from the UK IT services / MSP market they would not easily find themselves. No pitch. 4 sentences maximum.

*Touch 2 (Day 180):* Case study. A specific, anonymised outcome from a client similar to them. MSP size, headcount, ARR range, time to first managed service contract. No pitch. 6 sentences maximum.

*Touch 3 (Day 270):* Re-evaluation. A short, direct message acknowledging time has passed and asking a single yes/no question: "Is the new business pipeline situation at [Company] still something you're trying to solve, or has it sorted itself?"

**Hard rule:** If they don't respond to Touch 3: apply Walk-Away evaluation (Part 6).

---

### What Triggers Re-entry into Active Pursuit

Any one of these events triggers immediate re-entry into active pursuit (priority dial within 24 hours):

1. **They reply to a nurture email** — any reply, even brief.
2. **They like or comment on any of your LinkedIn content** — they are paying attention.
3. **They post about new business challenges, contract wins, team growth, margin pressure, or vendor changes on LinkedIn.**
4. **They post a new job ad for an engineer or account manager** — their pain and capacity need is active and visible.
5. **Their headcount grows** (from below-threshold back into ICP range, or from 10 toward 30 seats where scaling BD matters).
6. **A mutual contact refers them or mentions them in a context suggesting they're looking for what IGC offers.**
7. **They visit your website** (if you have tracking via HubSpot or Clarity — requires UTM-tagged links in nurture emails).

**On re-entry:** Do not restart the outreach calendar from Day 0. You are warm. Reference the last interaction. Call first if you have a number. DM if connected. Email if nothing else.

Opening for re-entry call:
> "Hi [Name], [Your name] from IGC — we spoke [approximate date] and I moved you to a longer-term follow-up. I'm calling because [specific re-engagement signal — they posted about BD, they liked your content, their engineer job ad went live]. Wanted to see if the timing had shifted at all."

---

### Monitoring Nurture Contacts for Re-engagement Signals

**Weekly checks (Monday, 15 minutes):**
1. Open HubSpot. Filter contacts: `Stage = Nurture`.
2. Cross-reference LinkedIn: check if any nurture contacts have posted in the last 7 days about BD, hiring, contract churn, margin, vendor changes, or MSP challenges.
3. Check Apollo or Cognism for any headcount changes in nurture companies.
4. Check for new job postings from nurture companies (set up Google Alerts: `"[company name]" "engineer"` / `"account manager"` / `"service desk"`, or use Apollo's change detection).

**HubSpot automation (set up once):**
- Email opened from a nurture contact → task created: "Nurture contact opened email — consider re-engagement."
- Calendly booking from any contact in nurture → automatic stage change to `Active Pursuit` + notification to owner.

## PART 6: WALK-AWAY RULES

### Hard Disqualifiers — Immediate Stop, No Re-approach

1. **Company closed or in administration.** Check Companies House if signal is strong. HubSpot: `Disqualified — company closed`. No re-approach.

2. **Contact has left the company.** If you called and were told "[Name] no longer works here": update contact record. Find their successor. The company itself may still be a prospect — do not disqualify the company, only the contact. Find the new MD/director and start a fresh sequence.

3. **Hostile response — requested removal.** Any explicit "take me off your list", "stop contacting us", "this is harassment." Hard stop, all channels, permanently. HubSpot: `Disqualified — requested removal`. Add to suppression list.

4. **They have merged with a larger firm or been acquired.** If the company is now part of a group exceeding 50 headcount or a national MSP platform: they are outside ICP. Disqualify.

5. **They have hired a dedicated sales director or BD person.** They have solved the problem you solve. They are no longer your prospect. HubSpot: `Disqualified — sales director or dedicated BD hire confirmed`. Set re-approach if the BD person leaves (monitor LinkedIn: if the BD hire's title disappears, re-engage the owner within 30 days).

6. **They are not a genuine MSP.** If it turns out they are IT staffing only, break-fix only with no managed service offering, or pure hardware reseller: outside ICP. Disqualify.

---

### Soft Disqualifiers — Condition-Based

**Soft disqualifier 1:** 12+ touches across 270 days with zero engagement (no open, no reply, no callback, no LinkedIn interaction). This is not hostility — it is total apathy. 

**Action:** Send Walk-Away email (see below). HubSpot: `Disqualified — engagement failure`. Set re-approach task for 12 months.

**Soft disqualifier 2:** They engaged once (one call, one email reply) but have been completely unresponsive for 180+ days despite nurture touches. This is not a real prospect — it is a name in a database.

**Action:** Walk-Away email. HubSpot: `Disqualified — cold post engagement`. Set re-approach 6 months.

**Soft disqualifier 3:** The company has shrunk below 5 headcount (seen in Apollo/LinkedIn). Below ICP threshold — survival mode. Disqualify until they grow back.

**Soft disqualifier 4:** The company has grown above 50 headcount. Likely has or will hire a dedicated BD/sales director. Out of ICP. Disqualify.

---

### The Walk-Away Record in HubSpot

When formally disqualifying a contact:

1. Set `igc_outreach_stage` = `disqualified`.
2. Set `Lifecycle stage` = `Other`.
3. Set `Deal stage` = `Closed Lost`.
4. Required note format:
   ```
   DISQUALIFIED: [Date]
   Reason: [Hard / Soft — specific reason]
   Re-approach date: [Date or "Never"]
   Last engagement: [Date and nature of engagement]
   Total touches: [Number]
   ```
5. Create a follow-up task for the re-approach date (even if 12 months out). Set it and don't think about it again.
6. Add to HubSpot suppression list for active outreach sequences.

---

### Re-Approach Timeline After Formal Disqualification

| Disqualification reason | Re-approach minimum |
|---|---|
| Hard no / requested removal | 12 months minimum. Only if offer has materially changed. |
| Post-verbal ghost | 90 days |
| 12-touch engagement failure | 12 months |
| 180-day cold post-engagement | 6 months |
| Company too small (< 5 headcount) | When they return to 5+ headcount |
| Company too large (> 50 headcount) | Never (out of ICP) |
| Company acquired / merged | Never (unless spun back out as independent) |
| Contact left company | Find new contact at company immediately |
| Sales director / BD hire confirmed | When BD hire leaves (monitor LinkedIn) |
| Not a genuine MSP (staffing/break-fix only) | Never |

---

### The Farewell Message

**Send a farewell message when:** The prospect has had at least one genuine two-way interaction (a call where they engaged, an email exchange of 2+ messages) and is now being formally disqualified after a long period of non-response.

**Do NOT send a farewell message when:** The prospect was always cold, never engaged, and is being disqualified after a sequence with zero response. Silence is the appropriate exit.

*Farewell message (email — for warm prospects being disqualified after engagement):*

*Subject:* `Closing the loop — [Company]`

> Hi [Name],
>
> It's been [timeframe] since we last spoke, and I haven't heard back from you on [last point of contact].
>
> I'm going to close out your record on my end and stop the follow-ups. No pressure — the timing clearly hasn't worked out and I'd rather be respectful of your inbox than keep appearing.
>
> If the new business pipeline situation at [Company] changes and the infrastructure conversation becomes relevant: I'm at [email]. Happy to pick it up.
>
> Best of luck with the MSP.
>
> [Your name] / IGC

**One send. No follow-up to the farewell.**

- Call date
- Call notes (their situation, pain, stated objections, level of interest: high/medium/low)
- Call outcome: `Proposal to follow` / `Not ready — nurture` / `Not fit — disqualify` / `Closed on call`
- Decision-maker confirmed (yes/no)

**Automated action:** If outcome = `Proposal to follow`: task created "Send proposal within 24 hours."

**Stuck deal alert:** Deal in Stage 6 for > 2 days with no outcome recorded → alert: "Call Held — record outcome."

---

### Stage 7: Proposal Sent

**Definition:** Agreement and invoice sent to prospect.

**Required fields added:**
- Proposal send date
- Proposal value (£3,500 build / build + retainer commitment noted)
- Payment split offered (yes/no)
- Enhanced guarantee offered (yes/no)

**Automated action:** Task: "Follow up on proposal at 48 hours."

**Stuck deal alert:** Deal in Stage 7 for > 5 business days → alert: "Proposal stale — follow up or walk away." This is the most important stuck deal alert in the pipeline.

---

### Stage 8: Closed Won

**Definition:** Agreement signed AND build fee received. Build starts within 48 hours.

**Required fields added:**
- Close date
- Build start date
- Assigned to delivery team / consultant
- Payment confirmation reference

**Automated action:**
- Notification: "New client — build starting [date]. [Company], [Contact], [Build slot number]."
- Task: "Send kickoff call invite — Day 1 of build."
- Update: `igc_outreach_stage` = `client`.
- Move contact to Client HubSpot pipeline (separate from sales pipeline).

---

### Stage 9: Closed Lost

**Definition:** Prospect will not proceed — price, timing, competitor chosen, or unresponsive after full sequence.

**Required fields added:**
- Lost reason (exact: price / timing / competitor / disqualified / ghost)
- Last contact date
- Re-approach date (if applicable)
- Notes for re-approach (what changed, what to reference)

**Automated action:** If re-approach date set: task created for that date. If `disqualified` flag: add to suppression list.

---

### Stage 10: Nurture

**Definition:** Prospect is warm or has expressed future interest but is not ready for active pursuit now.

**Required fields:**
- Nurture reason (genuine deferral / timing / sequence exhausted / post-soft-no)
- Re-engage trigger date or event
- Nurture touch schedule (quarterly)

**Automated action:** Task created for first nurture touch at 90 days.

**Stuck deal alert:** Deal in Nurture for > 120 days with no nurture touch sent → alert: "Nurture contact overdue for touch."

## PART 8: VOLUME MANAGEMENT RULES

### New Prospects Per Week

**Hard rule: 10 new prospects enter the active pursuit sequence per week.**

This is not an aspiration. It is the operational capacity constraint that protects the quality of every touchpoint.

At 10 new prospects per week:
- Week 1: 10 prospects in active sequence
- Week 4: 40 prospects in active sequence simultaneously
- Week 8: 50 prospects in active sequence (earlier cohorts move to nurture, new ones enter)

Maximum simultaneous active prospects: **50.** Above 50, the quality of follow-up deteriorates. Calls become rushed. DMs become generic. Close rates drop.

---

### Maximum Simultaneous Active Prospects: 50

**Why 50 and not more:**

An active prospect requires:
- LinkedIn monitoring (accepted? replied?)
- Call attempts (3 per active sequence, 5–12 minutes each including prep and logging)
- Email follow-up (tracking opens, drafting personalised lines)
- HubSpot logging after every touch (5 minutes minimum)
- Decision point evaluation at each stage

50 active prospects = approximately 3–4 hours of daily outreach activity at full capacity. Above this, one of three things happens: logging becomes sloppy (pipeline loses accuracy), calls become rushed (conversion drops), or hours become unsustainable.

---

### Pipeline Full — Prioritisation Rules

When active prospect count hits 50, no new prospects enter the active sequence until existing prospects either:
- Move to Nurture (sequence completed, no response)
- Move to Closed Won (signed)
- Move to Closed Lost (disqualified)

**Prioritisation order when capacity opens:**

1. **Re-engagement pipeline first.** Nurture contacts who have triggered a re-engagement signal always jump the queue ahead of cold new prospects. They are warmer and convert at higher rates.

2. **Tier 1 cold prospects second.** New Tier 1 contacts (4+ signals, active engineer/account manager job posting) get priority over Tier 2 and Tier 3.

3. **Tier 2 cold prospects third.**

4. **Tier 3 cold prospects last.** If pipeline is frequently full before reaching Tier 3, deprioritise Tier 3 outreach until Tier 1 and 2 are exhausted.

---

### Market Burn Rate — Protecting the UK Addressable Market

**UK ICP pool: ~500–800 qualified contacts (post-filter Tier 1–3).**

At 10 new prospects per week, the cold contact pool is exhausted in approximately 50–80 weeks. By Month 6, the first cohort of 200+ prospects is either won, in nurture, or can be re-approached.

**Protection rules:**

**Rule 1:** Do not run more than 10 new cold prospects per week. The urge to accelerate is counterproductive: burning through 30 prospects a week means running out of Tier 1 contacts in 3–4 months with no nurture pipeline built.

**Rule 2:** Keep Tier 3 contacts in reserve. Tier 3 (base filter match only, no additional signals) is the long tail. Do not start Tier 3 outreach until Tier 1 and 2 are fully worked (replied, nurture, or disqualified).

**Rule 3:** The nurture pipeline is a second crop. By Month 6, 100+ contacts are in nurture. Monthly re-engagement signals from this group are a predictable source of new opportunities without touching any cold contacts.

**Rule 4:** IT industry directories are supplementary crops. MSP Alliance, CompTIA Channel directory, Cyber Essentials registries, and UK Microsoft Partner directories are not in Apollo's primary pull. Use them for Q3/Q4 when the Apollo list is worked down.

**Rule 5:** Referrals are channel zero. Any client who completes a successful build (system live, conversations happening) is asked once, directly:
> "Do you know two other MSP owners who have the same new business problem you had 3 months ago? I'd value an introduction more than any marketing spend."

A referral jumps every prioritisation queue and enters active pursuit the same day received.

### Weekly Rhythm

**Monday:**
- Review HubSpot pipeline: identify stuck deals, stuck nurture touches, responses from last week's emails.
- Check LinkedIn: any connection acceptances or DM replies from the weekend.
- Review nurture contacts: any re-engagement signals (new engineer/account manager job postings, LinkedIn activity, headcount changes).
- Add 10 new prospects to the sequence (send connection requests).

**Tuesday–Thursday:**
- Call block: 10:00–12:00 BST (2 hours). Work through call queue from HubSpot task list. Aim: 8–12 calls per session.
- Second call block if needed: 16:00–17:30 BST.
- Respond to LinkedIn DMs and emails same day.
- HubSpot logging: update all touched prospects before end of day.

**Friday:**
- Send Friday nurture emails (better open rates Monday–Thursday, but Friday works for the "quiet reflection" email style of nurture content).
- Review the week: how many new connections? how many DM replies? how many calls connected? how many booked?
- Log the weekly numbers: `Connections sent / Accepted (%) / DMs replied / Calls attempted / Calls connected / Positive responses / Booked`.
- Update HubSpot tasks for next week.

### The Numbers That Tell You the Machine is Working

Track weekly. If any metric drops below threshold for 2 consecutive weeks: diagnose and fix before adding more volume.

| Metric | Healthy threshold |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn connection acceptance rate | ≥ 25% |
| Step 1 DM reply rate (of those connected) | ≥ 8% |
| Cold call connect rate (answered / attempted) | ≥ 18% |
| Cold call positive engagement rate (engaged / answered) | ≥ 30% |
| Email open rate | ≥ 20% |
| Email reply rate | ≥ 4% |
| Booked-to-held call ratio | ≥ 75% (≥ 3 in 4 booked calls actually happen) |
| Discovery-to-proposal conversion | ≥ 60% |
| Proposal-to-close conversion | ≥ 35% |
