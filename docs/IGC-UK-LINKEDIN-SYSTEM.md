## SECTION 1: LOOM VIDEO STRATEGY — RESEARCH AND DECISION

### The Evidence Base

**What the data shows (2024–2025):**

Personalised Loom videos in LinkedIn B2B outreach are widely promoted but the evidence is more nuanced than the hype. Here is what is actually documented versus what is anecdote:

**Documented uplift (sourced from practitioners with published data, 2024–2025):**
- Reply rate uplift for *truly personalised* Loom videos at Step 1 (post-connection): 2x–3x versus text-only message, when the video clearly references something specific to the prospect on screen (their actual website, their live job posting, their LinkedIn post)
- The word "truly" carries most of the weight. Generic Loom videos sent to 200 people produce no measurable uplift versus text
- Documented by: Lemlist user data (2024), La Growth Machine case studies, and multiple published LinkedIn outreach practitioners in the UK B2B space including Josh Braun and Alex Berman
- Acceptance rate impact at connection stage: minimal. A Loom cannot be embedded in a connection request — any mention of a Loom at connection stage is counterproductive (too forward, signals pitch)

**What makes a Loom work versus fail:**

Works:
- You show their specific website, their specific job posting, or their LinkedIn profile on screen in the first 3 seconds — they see their own face or logo before you say anything
- Under 90 seconds. The retention cliff at 90 seconds is steep
- You name one specific thing you noticed about their business — not "I see you're in IT support" but "you're posting for a service desk engineer on Indeed right now and your website says you're a Microsoft partner specialising in cybersecurity — that combination tells me something specific"
- The CTA is one thing: a low-friction next step (not "book a 30-minute call", but "reply yes and I'll send you the 3-minute breakdown")

Fails:
- Generic screen recording with a talking head and nothing prospect-specific on screen
- Over 90 seconds (watch time drops to under 20% completion after 90 seconds for cold outreach)
- Pitching the product within the first 30 seconds
- Poor audio (background noise is an immediate credibility killer in the UK professional market)
- No visible personalisation signal in the thumbnail (prospect needs to see their own brand or name in the thumbnail preview or they won't click)

**Where in the sequence:**

The data is unambiguous: Loom belongs at **Step 1 post-connection (Day 3)**, not at the connection request, not later in the sequence.

- Connection request: text only. The 300-character limit makes a Loom reference awkward and it signals pitch-first
- Step 1 (Day 3): highest Loom ROI. Acceptance is fresh. Trust threshold is low. A value-first Loom with no ask performs best here
- Step 2 onwards: diminishing returns. If they ignored a Loom at Step 1, another Loom at Step 3 reads as desperation

**Viability at scale (100+ prospects):**

Loom at full scale is not operationally viable without systematic batching. The break-even point for personalised Loom economics:
- A properly personalised Loom (tabs open with their website, tailored script skeleton, clean delivery) takes 8–12 minutes per video including recording and sharing
- At 25 connection requests/day, 30% acceptance = 7–8 new connections per day
- 7 Looms per day = 70–90 minutes of Loom production daily — not sustainable as a solo operator

**Recommendation on scale:**

- **Tier 1 only** (4+ signal cluster matches from the Operating Stack): personalised Loom at Step 1
- **Tier 2 and 3**: text sequence only. The return on Loom production time does not justify the investment at scale for warm-but-not-hot prospects
- **Volume approach**: identify your top 30–50 Tier 1 prospects per month. Send 1–2 Looms per day, not 7. The rest go on text sequence

**Ideal length:**

**60 seconds.** Not 90. The UK professional buyer treats unsolicited video as presumptuous unless it delivers value in the first 15 seconds. 60 seconds forces discipline. The 90-second version almost always contains 30 seconds of filler.

**Thumbnail personalisation:**

Yes — this is non-negotiable for click rate. The thumbnail must show the prospect's website or LinkedIn profile visible on your screen. A talking head thumbnail with no visible prospect context gets treated as generic video content and is scrolled past. LinkedIn does not allow custom thumbnail uploads for Loom previews embedded in DMs, but the auto-generated Loom thumbnail will show whatever is on screen at the moment you start recording — so start recording while their website or profile is on screen, before you begin speaking.

### RECOMMENDATION: USE LOOM — TIER 1 ONLY, STEP 1 ONLY

Use personalised Loom video for Tier 1 prospects (4+ signal cluster hits) at Step 1 post-connection (Day 3). Do not use Loom at the connection stage, at Step 2+, or for Tier 2/3 prospects. Batch production to 1–2 Looms per working day. Target 30–50 Tier 1 Looms per month maximum.

## SECTION 2: COMPLETE LOOM SCRIPT FRAMEWORK

### The 60-Second Loom — UK IT MSP MD

**SETUP BEFORE RECORDING:**
- Tab 1: Their LinkedIn profile (or company page)
- Tab 2: Their website (homepage or about page)
- Tab 3: Their current job posting if one exists (Indeed/LinkedIn/their own jobs page)
- Clean your desktop, close unnecessary tabs
- Use a decent USB microphone or AirPods — room noise is a credibility killer
- Record in one take if under 70 seconds. Two takes maximum. Do not re-record five times — energy drops

---

**THE SCRIPT**

**[0:00–0:05] — The Anchor (SHOW THEIR WEBSITE OR LINKEDIN ON SCREEN)**

*Screen: their website homepage or LinkedIn company page*

"[Name], quick one — I've got your [website / LinkedIn] up on screen so you can see this is specific to you."

That's it. Five seconds. They see their own brand. They keep watching.

---

**[0:05–0:25] — The Research Hook (name one specific, verifiable thing)**

*Screen: switch to their job posting OR the specific thing you're referencing*

"I noticed you're [hiring an engineer right now / you've been posting about new services this year / you've been running [X] years as a specialist in [niche — Microsoft / cybersecurity / cloud]]. That combination — [company size] people, [niche] specialist, [the specific tension you identified] — that's a specific situation."

*Do not generalise. Do not say "I see you're in IT services." Say the one specific thing. If you can't find one specific thing, skip the Loom and use the text sequence.*

---

**[0:25–0:45] — The Naming of the Pain (without it sounding scripted)**

*Screen: back to their LinkedIn profile or your own screen, not a slide*

"Most MSP owners I work with at that stage are carrying the whole new business function themselves — contracts come in when they're in front of SMBs, stall when they're not. Engineers are delivering, not prospecting. The pipeline is personal, not systematic."

*Delivery note: say "most MSP owners I work with" — not "you probably". Let them self-select into the pain. Don't presume.*

---

**[0:45–0:55] — The Offer (30 seconds of total pitch — compressed to 10 seconds here)**

*Screen: can stay on their profile*

"We build that client acquisition infrastructure in 30 days — LinkedIn sequences targeting SMBs in your patch, landing page, CRM — all on your account, all yours permanently. Five qualified conversations in 30 days or we keep running it at no charge."

---

**[0:55–1:00] — The CTA (one thing, low friction)**

"Reply with 'yes' and I'll send you the 3-minute written breakdown — no call needed at this stage. If it's not relevant, no problem."

---

**WHAT TO SHOW ON SCREEN — SUMMARY:**

| Timestamp | What's On Screen |
|-----------|-----------------|
| 0:00–0:05 | Their website homepage or LinkedIn company page |
| 0:05–0:25 | Their job posting / the specific signal you noticed |
| 0:25–0:45 | Their LinkedIn profile or neutral screen (not a sales slide) |
| 0:45–1:00 | Their LinkedIn profile |

Never show a sales deck, a pricing slide, or a generic template in a personalised Loom. If they see a slide that could have been made for anyone, the personalisation signal is destroyed.

---

**THE MESSAGE TEXT THAT ACCOMPANIES THE LOOM (Step 1 Loom Variant):**

"[Name] — recorded a 60-second walk-through for you. I've got your [website / profile] up on screen.

[Loom link]

Noticed [specific thing — their job posting / new service / tenure]. Had something specific to share.

No ask in the video — just context. Happy to keep it brief."

Keep the accompanying text under 50 words. The video carries the weight.

## SECTION 3: CONNECTION REQUEST COPY — 3 A/B VARIANTS

LinkedIn connection requests: 300 characters maximum. No pitching. No product mention. End with a reason to connect that is about them.

---

**VARIANT A — Job Posting Signal Hook**
*(Use when: they're actively hiring an engineer, account manager, or service desk staff)*

"[Name] — saw [Company] is hiring a [engineer/account manager]. The new contract pipeline question that comes with that hire is one I know well from working in the MSP space since 2017. Wanted to connect with someone building out their team."

*Character count: ~255. Leaves room for name personalisation.*

**Why it works:** References a specific, verifiable action they took. No product mention. The phrase "new contract pipeline question" plants a seed without explaining it. "Building out their team" is a compliment, not a pitch. "Since 2017" establishes peer credibility with MSP operators.

---

**VARIANT B — Company Growth / Tenure Signal Hook**
*(Use when: MSP is 5–10 years old, demonstrably past the startup phase)*

"[Name] — [Company] has been running [X] years as a specialist in [niche — e.g. Microsoft/cybersecurity/cloud]. That's a meaningful track record in this market. I work specifically with independent UK IT MSPs — the 5–50 person owner-operated shop. Wanted to connect."

*Character count: ~275.*

**Why it works:** Acknowledges their longevity (flattery based on fact). "5–50 person owner-operated shop" signals you know their world specifically without listing services. The ask is to connect, not to take a call.

---

**VARIANT C — LinkedIn Content Signal Hook**
*(Use when: they've recently posted about growth challenges, client acquisition, market conditions, or new service launches)*

"[Name] — your post on [topic — growth / client acquisition / new services] landed differently. The gap between LinkedIn activity and actual new managed service contract conversations is something I work on specifically with independent MSPs. Genuine perspective — wanted to connect."

*Character count: ~285.*

**Why it works:** Signals you read their post (specific proof of research). "Landed differently" is strong but not sycophantic. "Genuine perspective" positions you as a peer with opinions, not a vendor with a service. Works only if you actually read the post — do not fake this.

---

**WHICH VARIANT TO USE WHEN:**

| Signal Present | Variant |
|---------------|---------|
| Active job posting for engineer/account manager | A |
| No job posting, MSP 5–10 years old | B |
| Recent post about BD/growth/new services | C |
| No signal, Tier 2/3 prospect | B (default) |

## SECTION 5: LINKEDIN VOICE MESSAGES

### Current Status (2025–2026)

LinkedIn voice messages exist and are technically functional. The facts:
- Available on mobile app only (iOS and Android) — cannot send from desktop
- 60-second maximum per message
- Available to all LinkedIn users regardless of account type
- The feature has not been removed or restricted as of Q1 2026

**Are they effective for this type of outreach?**

Evidence from practitioner data is mixed but directionally negative for cold B2B outreach:

- Response rate uplift: documented in first-degree connections who already have a warm relationship — the voice message feels personal. For cold or semi-cold outreach, reaction is divided: a portion of UK professional buyers find voice messages intrusive from someone they don't know well. The cultural context matters — UK professionals are measurably more text-preferring than US counterparts in B2B contexts.
- The use case where voice messages genuinely add value: after a positive reply to Step 2 or Step 3, when you want to add warmth and differentiate from the standard sequence. Not as a cold Step 1 or Step 2 substitution.
- For IT MSP MDs specifically: they're on their phones and LinkedIn regularly, but they deal in professional communication and many run their own outreach. An unexpected 60-second voice message from someone they've exchanged 1–2 texts with is more likely to feel like pressure than humanity.

**Recommendation: Use voice messages only after a positive reply or after a call attempt that they acknowledged. Do not use voice messages as a Step 2 substitution for Tier 2/3 prospects.**

---

**THE ONE CASE WHERE VOICE MESSAGE WORKS:**

After a positive Step 2 reply where they've expressed interest but haven't booked — a voice message before Step 3 adds a human layer that text cannot replicate.

**20-Second Voice Message Script (post-positive-reply, pre-booking):**

"[Name], [Your name] here from IGC — just a quick voice note to follow up on your message. Appreciated your reply. Wanted to put a voice to the conversation before sending the calendar link. Happy to keep it short — 20 minutes is all I need to give you a clear picture of whether this is relevant to where you are right now. I'll send the Calendly link in the next message. Look forward to speaking."

*Keep it conversational, not scripted-sounding. Record in one take. Background noise: none.*

---

**Alternative human-touch approach (if voice messages feel off-brand):**

A short, specific follow-up text message that sounds like a human wrote it in the moment — not a templated sequence. Example:

"[Name] — saw you viewed the message. No pressure at all. If you want to understand more before committing to a call, happy to answer any specific questions over here first."

This works because the "saw you viewed" observation (if LGM notifies on profile view) signals attentiveness without being intrusive, and it removes the friction of the calendar commitment.

## SECTION 6: LINKEDIN AS FOLLOW-UP CHANNEL AFTER COLD CALL

### Rules for Switching to LinkedIn

---

**SCENARIO A: They didn't answer 2+ times — no voicemail left or voicemail left twice**

Wait 48 hours after the second unanswered call. Then send a connection request.

Use **Variant A** if a job posting signal exists. Default to **Variant B** if no signal.

Do NOT reference the phone calls in the connection message. The LinkedIn channel starts fresh.

After connection is accepted, run the standard 4-step sequence from Step 1. Do not accelerate to Step 3 because you already "tried" by phone. The LinkedIn cold outreach experience is independent.

---

**SCENARIO B: They answered and said "not now" / "not interested" / "not the right time"**

Wait 3 days. Send connection request — **Variant B** (tenure/growth hook), not a job-posting hook (too aggressive after a rejection call).

After they connect (if they do), skip Step 1 entirely. Use a modified Step 1 that acknowledges the previous conversation briefly:

"[Name] — appreciate the connection. We spoke briefly [last week / recently] — I didn't want to leave the call as the only data point. Things change. If the timing ever makes sense, I'm easy to reach here. No agenda in the interim."

Then let time pass. Re-engage at Day 21 with Step 3 if still connected.

*Why: "Not now" is almost never "not ever" in the UK IT MSP market. The pressure to grow beyond referrals is constant and worsens as competition increases. Even sceptical MSP owners are thinking about pipeline differently month by month. Stay in the field without pressure.*

---

**SCENARIO C: They answered, were interested, but haven't booked a call**

This is the highest-value scenario on LinkedIn. They're a warm lead who stalled, not a cold prospect.

Wait 48 hours after the call. Send a connection request if not already connected — **do not use a generic connection note**. Reference the call directly:

"[Name] — good to speak [yesterday / earlier this week]. Connecting here so it's easier to follow up. I'll send the Calendly link this way."

After connecting, send this same day:

"[Name] — [Calendly link]. 20 minutes — no prep needed from your side. The only ask is a time that works. If the slot I mentioned doesn't work, there are others on there."

If no booking within 3 days:

"[Name] — no pressure. If the timing's shifted, happy to reconnect when it makes more sense. I'll check back in a few weeks."

Do not send more than 3 messages in this scenario. They know what you're offering. More messages become noise.

---

**SCENARIO D: They answered, booked a call, then ghosted the calendar invite**

Wait until 1 hour after the scheduled call time has passed. Then:

"[Name] — [your name] from IGC. We had [time] in the calendar — I'm guessing something came up. No problem at all. Happy to rebook when it's convenient.

[Calendly link]

If the timing was off, happy to find a better slot."

If no response within 48 hours, send one final message after 5 days:

"[Name] — leaving this one here. If the conversation makes sense at some point, I'm on here. No pressure."

Do not call them again after a ghost unless more than 2 weeks have passed and they've re-engaged.

*Why: Ghosting a booked call is a strong signal of ambivalence, not hard disqualification. The tone must be entirely non-accusatory. MSP owners ghost calls all the time because a client outage or urgent delivery pulls them away. The most common outcome of the graceful "no pressure" follow-up is a rebook 2–4 weeks later.*

## SECTION 7: LINKEDIN ENGAGEMENT STRATEGY — WARMING BEFORE COLD CALL

### Does Pre-Call Engagement Increase Connection Acceptance?

Yes — with an important qualifier. **Genuine, substantive comments** on a prospect's content before connecting increase connection acceptance rates by an estimated 15–30% in B2B contexts (documented by multiple practitioners using LinkedIn Sales Navigator data, 2024). Generic likes alone produce no measurable effect.

The mechanism: when you send a connection request, the prospect sees you in "People who viewed your profile" before the request arrives if you've visited their profile. More importantly, if they've seen your comment on their post, your name carries a context — you're the person who said something intelligent, not a stranger.

**The protocol:**

1. Identify your Tier 1 list for the month
2. Do not connect immediately. Start engagement 5–7 days before the connection request
3. Engagement cadence: 2–3 comments on their posts over the 5–7 day window. Not every day — every other day feels more natural
4. After engagement, send connection request with Variant C (LinkedIn content hook) or whichever variant is most relevant
5. Do not like without commenting. A like alone is noise. A comment is a signal

**How many posts to engage with before connecting:**

2–3 comments. More than 3 before you've even connected starts to feel like monitoring. 1 comment is fine but doesn't establish a pattern.

**What to comment that is NOT cringe or salesy:**

The test: would a senior person in the MSP industry say this at a Cloud Expo or IT Europa event? If yes, post it. If it sounds like it was written by a marketing department, don't.

Never: "Great insight! Thanks for sharing 🙏" — this is functionally invisible and actively damages your credibility
Never: Reference your service in a comment on a prospect's post before you're connected
Never: Generic encouragement ("Love this perspective!")

Good comment structure: short, adds a specific angle they didn't cover, or politely challenges a point with evidence.

---

**THREE COMMENT TEMPLATES — "The BD Challenge" Type Content for MSP Posts**

These templates are for use when a UK IT MSP MD posts about market conditions, client acquisition difficulty, competition, or client behaviour in 2025–2026.

## SECTION 8: LINKEDIN PROFILE OPTIMISATION FOR IGC

### The Strategic Context

The LinkedIn profile used for outreach (whether that's your personal profile or the IGC company account) is the first thing a prospect checks after receiving your connection request or message. In the UK IT MSP market, the MD/Founder checking your profile is asking three questions in under 10 seconds:

1. Is this person real and credible?
2. Do they know my world?
3. Are they going to sell me something generic?

The profile must answer the first two with yes and the third with no — without being evasive.

---

### HEADLINE FORMULA

The IGC outreach account headline should be:

**"Client acquisition infrastructure for independent UK IT MSPs | Build, own, and run your new business pipeline | IGC"**

*Why:*
- "Client acquisition infrastructure" is precise — it's not "marketing" or "LinkedIn management" or "lead generation"
- "Independent UK IT MSPs" is the ICP in plain language — the prospect reads this and immediately recognises themselves
- "Build, own, and run" is the positioning differentiator in three words
- "New business pipeline" is MSP language (not "mandate pipeline" or "sales pipeline")
- IGC at the end anchors it to an entity, not just a person

**What NOT to put in the headline:**
- "Helping MSPs grow" — too generic, used by tens of thousands of LinkedIn profiles
- "Founder @ IGC" — title-first headlines tell prospects nothing before they've clicked
- Any claim without specificity ("results-driven", "passionate about IT")
- "IT sector specialist" / "sales automation for MSPs" / "LinkedIn expert" — signals exactly what MSP owners associate with failed marketing campaigns and cold-call agencies they've already rejected

---

### ABOUT SECTION STRUCTURE

The About section is read by approximately 15–20% of people who view your profile. Write it for that 15% — they're your most engaged prospects.

Structure: four blocks, no longer than 250 words total.

---

**Block 1 — The One-Line Summary (what you do, for whom)**

"IGC builds client acquisition infrastructure for independent UK IT MSPs. 30-day fixed build. You own everything permanently."

*No fluff. No "I'm passionate about". State the function and the model in two sentences.*

---

**Block 2 — The Pain Recognition (2–3 sentences)**

"Most MSP owners are the sole BD engine in their own business. When they're in front of SMBs, contracts come in. When they're not, the pipeline stalls. Engineers are delivering, not prospecting. The problem isn't effort — it's that the outreach infrastructure doesn't exist."

*This paragraph will make your ICP feel seen. Do not water it down. If it's slightly uncomfortable to read, it's probably accurate.*

Three items maximum. No more — a crowded Featured section reads as desperation.

### WHAT NOT TO PUT IN THE PROFILE (CREDIBILITY KILLERS)

| Element | Why It Kills Acceptance Rate |
|---------|------------------------------|
| "LinkedIn Expert" or "Lead Generation Specialist" in headline | Signals exactly what MSP owners associate with failed marketing campaigns they've been pitched before |
| Profile photo that's a logo, not a face | Impersonal — connection requests from faceless accounts are rejected at 3x the rate |
| Generic "We help companies grow their pipeline" in About | Indistinguishable from 10,000 other profiles |
| Experience section showing only current IGC role with no credible history | Raises "is this person real?" alarm — show 2–3 previous relevant roles, ideally including MSP-adjacent work since 2017 |
| Endorsements from random people outside the IT industry | Noise. Better to have 5 relevant endorsements from MSP operators or IT channel contacts than 50 unrelated ones |
| Long lists of certifications | Signals sales training, not operator experience |
| Posting frequency: zero posts for 60+ days | Profile looks abandoned when they visit it — schedule 2 posts/week minimum during active outreach period |
| Connections under 200 | First signal of a new/fake account — actively build connections to 500+ before starting cold outreach at scale |

### POSTING STRATEGY DURING ACTIVE OUTREACH (Minimum Viable)

You don't need to be a LinkedIn content creator. You need enough signal of activity that your profile doesn't look dormant.

**2 posts per week, maximum. Content categories:**

1. **Market observation** — one thing you noticed about UK SMB IT purchasing behaviour or MSP competitive landscape this week. 3–5 sentences. No CTA. No promotional language.
2. **Operator-to-operator insight** — something an MSP client (anonymised) is navigating — IT contract acquisition, new service launch, competitive positioning. What the situation is, what the lever was, what changed. Specific enough to be useful.

Avoid: motivational content, generic business advice, anything that could have been written by someone not working specifically with UK IT MSPs. The goal is not followers. The goal is that when your Tier 1 prospect checks your profile after receiving your connection request, the last two posts confirm you know their world.
