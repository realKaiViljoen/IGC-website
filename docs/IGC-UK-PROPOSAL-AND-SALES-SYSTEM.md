### PAGE 2 — THE SYSTEM

---

**What we build**

**1. LinkedIn Outreach Sequences**

Two active sequences run simultaneously from your LinkedIn account, cloud-based (no browser extension, no laptop required to be on). Sequence A targets MDs and Managing Directors at 10–100 employee businesses in your geographic area. Sequence B targets Finance Directors and Operations Directors at 50–200 employee businesses. Each sequence runs 5 touchpoints over 21 days: a connection request, then four direct messages timed to build context before asking for anything. 20–25 connection requests per day during business hours. 10 message templates, all written in your voice based on your kickoff call, all reviewed and approved by you before going live.

**2. Client Landing Page**

A dedicated page on your domain built for one purpose: booking a discovery call with you. Above the fold: who you are, what managed services you provide, and a Calendly embed. Below: three anonymised client wins (type of business, IT challenge solved, outcome — e.g. "reduced downtime by 90%, achieved Cyber Essentials certification"), social proof, and a second call-to-action. No "passionate about technology" language. No generic IT agency speak. Written operator-to-operator, because that's the audience. When your LinkedIn sequence generates interest, this is where prospects land to self-qualify and book.

**3. HubSpot CRM Pipeline**

A dedicated "Client Acquisition" pipeline inside HubSpot (free tier, your account). Seven stages: Prospect → Connected → Replied → Call Booked → Call Held → Proposal Sent → Active Contract. Custom properties configured: LinkedIn profile URL, business size, IT situation (break-fix / unhappy with MSP / growing), sequence step, estimated contract value. The CRM tracks where every prospect sits and what happens next. Not your service desk ticketing system. Your new managed service contract pipeline, only.

**4. Automation Workflows**

Make.com (approximately £9/month on your account) bridges your LinkedIn automation tool and HubSpot. When a connection request is accepted, a contact is created in HubSpot automatically. When a reply comes in, the stage updates and you receive a task notification. When a prospect books a call via Calendly, HubSpot moves them to Call Booked and creates a prep task. You stop managing information manually. The system surfaces the things that need your attention.

**5. Reporting Dashboard**

Eight metrics tracked in a HubSpot native dashboard: connections sent, acceptance rate, reply rate, new CRM contacts this week, stage movements, calls booked, calls held (the guarantee tracker), and active contracts. You have a bookmark link. You check it yourself. IGC sends a Loom video update every Friday during the build month — under 5 minutes, covering what the numbers say and what we're adjusting.

## SECTION 2: HOW TO DELIVER THE PROPOSAL

### The Evidence on Loom vs PDF vs Shared Doc

**What 2024–2025 B2B data shows:**

The research on video-assisted proposal delivery is directionally consistent across multiple B2B services studies:

- Proposals accompanied by a personalised video walkthrough close at approximately 26–35% higher rates than email PDF alone (Proposify B2B State of Proposals 2024; Better Proposals conversion data 2024)
- The effect is strongest in mid-market B2B services (£2,000–£15,000 deal sizes) — which is exactly where IGC sits
- Video proposals increase "time spent reviewing" by 2–3x, which directly correlates with close rate
- The UK B2B buyer preference data (B2B International, 2024) shows that UK buyers rate "personalised, relevant communication" as the #1 trust signal from a new vendor — generic PDF performs worst on this metric
- DocSend 2024 analysis: proposals with a video companion have a 41% lower "forwarded without opening" rate — meaning more decision-makers actually watch vs. skim-and-ignore

**Recommendation: Option B — Loom walkthrough + PDF**

Do not use a shared Google Doc as the primary vehicle. It signals "I'm still working on this." The PDF signals completeness. The Loom signals this was made specifically for them.

The formula:

1. Send the email with both the Loom link (first) and PDF attached (second)
2. Subject line makes clear it's personalised: "IGC Proposal for [MSP Name] — walkthrough inside"
3. Loom is 3–4 minutes. Not longer. UK buyers will not watch 8 minutes.
4. PDF is the leave-behind they forward to a business partner

Tracking: use DocSend (not just Loom analytics) to track PDF opens, time-on-page by section, and re-opens. This tells you exactly where they're spending time and whether a partner is reviewing it.

### THE BUILD FEE (£3,500)

**Is it negotiable?**

No, as a standard position. Yes, under one specific condition: the prospect commits to a 3-month retainer upfront before work starts. In that case — and only that case — the build fee can be reduced to £2,750. This is not a discount. It is a risk-adjusted trade: guaranteed retainer revenue offsets reduced build fee. Never offer this pre-emptively. It exists to close deals that would otherwise not close.

**The floor:** £2,750, with 3-month upfront retainer commitment (£2,750 + £4,750 = £7,500 total Day 1 commitment — better economics for IGC than £3,500 with no retainer).

**What you give up to reduce it:** Nothing from scope. The work is identical. The reduction is pure financial trade for retainer commitment — make this explicit.

**Framing when asked "can you do it cheaper":**

"The build fee isn't priced to cover my time. It's priced to reflect what a working client acquisition pipeline is worth to an IT MSP — which is one new managed service contract at minimum. I won't reduce it because reducing it would make the guarantee economics unstable. What I can do is this: if you commit to three months of retainer upfront, I'll bring the build fee to £2,750. The total is higher but you're locking in the operating support that makes the system work long-term."

### THE GUARANTEE

**Can guarantee terms be negotiated?**

The threshold (5 conversations) is fixed. The definition of "qualified" is fixed as written. These are not moveable.

**If they want a stronger guarantee:**

"The guarantee as written is already uncomfortable for us — that's intentional. I'm not going to offer a money-back on the build fee because the build fee pays for 30 days of our work, which we've done whether or not the system generates 5 conversations in the window. What the guarantee covers is the ongoing service: you don't pay for the retainer until the system performs. That's the right way to structure a guarantee on infrastructure."

**If they want to define "qualified" differently:**

Listen to what they're proposing. If they want to add conditions that make it harder to qualify a conversation (e.g. "must have already issued an RFP"), decline: "Adding conditions to the definition makes the guarantee harder for you to collect on, not easier. The definition I've written is specific enough to be meaningful and achievable enough to be a real commitment. I'd rather keep it as is."

If they want to loosen the definition (e.g. "any reply counts"), also decline: "A reply that doesn't confirm an IT need isn't a qualified conversation — it could be someone asking me to remove them. I want the guarantee to mean something, so the definition needs to stay tight."

### THE SPECIAL OFFER

**What triggers the special offer:**

The special offer exists for one purpose: to close a deal that is genuine but stalled on price or commitment. Trigger conditions (all must be true):

1. The prospect has been on a discovery call and received a proposal
2. They have engaged with the follow-up — not gone cold
3. They have raised a financial or timing objection specifically (not "I need to think about it" as an avoidance)
4. It has been at least 5 days since proposal delivery
5. You believe the deal is closeable but the current structure is the blocker

Never use the special offer as an opener, as a pre-emptive tool, or more than once per prospect. If they reject the special offer, the deal is dead. Move on.

**Three special offer structures in order of preference:**

**Option A — Deferred retainer start (cheapest for IGC to offer)**

Standard build fee (£3,500), retainer start pushed back 30 days from the end of the build. This gives the prospect 60 days of system operation before their first retainer payment. Net cost to IGC: one month of deferred retainer revenue (~£1,750). Use this when the prospect's objection is "I want to see it work before I commit to the retainer."

Script: "Here's what I can do: the build starts at £3,500 as agreed. The retainer doesn't begin until day 60 — 30 days after the build ends. You get a full month of watching the system run before you decide to keep the operating support. If it's not working at day 60, you don't start the retainer. That's the only adjustment I'm willing to make."

**Option B — 3-month retainer bundle with reduced build fee (second preference)**

£2,750 build fee (reduced from £3,500) with a 3-month retainer commitment paid upfront at £4,750. Total Day 1 commitment: £7,500. Net to IGC over 3 months: £7,500 (vs. £3,500 + potential zero retainer). Better total economics. Use this when the prospect is serious about the retainer but resistant to the full build fee.

Script: "I'll reduce the build fee to £2,750 if you commit to three months of retainer upfront. That's £7,500 total today — you save £750 on the build, you're locked in for three months of operating support, and I have the revenue certainty to give this engagement full attention. The total is higher but the structure is better for both of us."

**Option C — Last resort: reduced build fee with no retainer discount (use once)**

£2,750 build fee, month-to-month retainer at standard rate, no conditions. This is the absolute floor. It signals flexibility and you do not want to signal flexibility. Use it only when the deal would otherwise be lost to a genuine budget constraint (not perceived value gap — that's a different problem). Never use this twice — word gets around.

Script: "I'm going to do something I don't typically do. £2,750 for the build, everything else as written. That's the only discount I'll offer and it's available until [DATE — 3–5 business days]. I'm doing this because I think we're a good fit and I'd rather get this deal done than hold a line that costs both of us."

**The deadline framing — how to create urgency without manipulation:**

Urgency is only legitimate when it reflects a real constraint. The constraints you have are real:

1. Build slot capacity: IGC takes a limited number of builds per quarter. If a slot fills, it fills.
2. Price validity: The proposal states 14-day validity. Use it.
3. Quarter close: End of quarter (March, June, September, December) is a natural window.

Script for build slot urgency: "I have [NUMBER] build slot[s] remaining this quarter. When that fills, the next availability is [DATE].
