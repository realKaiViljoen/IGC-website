## SECTION 1 — PRE-CALL RESEARCH PROTOCOL

**Time budget: 2 minutes per prospect.** Not 5. Not 10. Two minutes of focused signal gathering, then dial.

### The 2-Minute Research Checklist

Open three tabs simultaneously: LinkedIn profile, company website, Apollo record.

**LinkedIn (60 seconds)**

- How long have they been MD/Founder at this MSP? (Under 3 years = still building, still hungry. Over 8 years without a BD hire = the problem is structural, not temporary.)
- Are they posting on LinkedIn? What about? (Tech content — cybersecurity tips, Microsoft 365 explainers, cloud migration posts — means they've tried the "post and hope" credibility play with no BD engine behind it. Zero activity = they've checked out of LinkedIn entirely. Either creates an opening.)
- Who else is at the company on LinkedIn? (Is the MD the only visible person? Engineers, service desk analysts, a technical director? Any BD, sales, or account manager hire? No BD hire = confirmed pain point.)
- Any recent announcements? (New engineers, new services — cybersecurity launch, Microsoft 365 migration practice, vCIO offering — award wins, partner-tier uplifts, office moves. "Saw you just launched a cyber practice" is a real opener.)

**Company Website (30 seconds)**

- What managed IT services do they actually offer? (Full managed IT? Cyber? Cloud? Microsoft partner work? vCIO? Just helpdesk reselling?)
- Do they have client logos, case studies, testimonials? (If it's thin or it's the same three logos everyone uses, they're not winning contracts they can showcase — BD is the bottleneck.)
- Is there a "why choose us" section that sounds generic — "proactive", "responsive", "trusted partner"? (No differentiation means they're competing on price, not value — price-competing MSPs have the worst contract pipeline problem.)
- Job postings on their own site? (Hiring an engineer, account manager, service desk analyst, or project engineer = growth signal — they're building delivery capacity without a confirmed managed contract pipeline to fill it.)

**Apollo Record (30 seconds)**

- Verify: headcount, founded year, phone number type (mobile vs. direct dial)
- Note their tier score (Tier 1/2/3 — set before you start your dial block)
- Check `igc_last_dial_date` — have they been called before? Any notes?

### The 3 Signals That Make This a Tier 1 Call

You need a minimum of two to prioritise this call today:

1. **Open job posting for an engineer, account manager, or service desk analyst** — they're growing headcount without a confirmed contract pipeline to cover the salary
2. **Founded 2016–2022** — old enough to have scaled past pure referrals, young enough to still be owner-led on BD
3. **MD is visibly posting on LinkedIn** — about growth, new services, cyber threats, market challenges, or hiring — they know LinkedIn matters, they just don't have the infrastructure behind it

### The 3-Second Mental Model

Before you dial, complete this sentence in your head:

> *"This person is running a [X]-person IT MSP. They founded it in [year]. They are the sole BD engine. Right now, their [engineers are busy on delivery so no one is prospecting / they've been posting on LinkedIn without generating contract conversations / they're growing headcount without confirmed new contract pipeline]. Today is the right call because [specific signal]."*

If you can't complete it, do 60 more seconds of research or move to the next contact.

### What to Personalise in the Opening

The opening hook must reference ONE specific, verifiable observation. In order of strength:

1. They are **actively hiring an engineer, account manager, or service desk analyst** (check their website job board or LinkedIn jobs)
2. They **recently posted about a new service offering** (cybersecurity launch, Microsoft 365 migration practice, cloud migration, vCIO, Cyber Essentials practice)
3. They **recently won an award or achieved a certification** (Microsoft Solutions Partner, Cyber Essentials Plus, ISO 27001, a local business award)
4. They are **in a specific vertical niche** (legal tech MSP, healthcare IT, financial services IT, professional services IT) — easier to reference a specific sector pain

Do not use generic openers. "I noticed you're an MSP" is not a signal. "I saw you're hiring a second-line engineer" is.

## SECTION 2 — CALL SCRIPTS

### UNIVERSAL RULES BEFORE ANY SCRIPT

- **Speak at 70% of your natural pace for the first 10 seconds.** Most people hang up during a rushed opening.
- **Never sound like you're reading.** Internalise the logic; use the words as a scaffold.
- **Their first response tells you everything.** "Yeah, what is it?" = guarded (go to B). "Oh, interesting" = curious (go to A). "Not interested" in the first 5 seconds = usually premature, don't accept it immediately.
- **Silence is not dead air — it's their brain processing.** Ask a question, then wait. Do not fill the silence.
- **You are not selling on this call.** You are qualifying and booking a 20-minute discovery call. Nothing more.

---

### A. LIVE ANSWER — INTERESTED (They Engage Positively)

**Use when:** They respond to your opener with genuine curiosity, a confirmation of the pain, or questions.

---

**OPENING HOOK — Version 1 (Job Posting Signal)**

> "Hi [Name], my name is [Your name], calling from IGC. I saw [Company name] is hiring a [engineer / account manager / service desk analyst] — and I'll be honest, that's exactly the situation where I call. Most MSP owners I speak to at that point are thinking about where the new managed service contracts are coming from to support that growth. Is that on your radar at all?"

*Wait for their response. Do not continue until they speak.*

**OPENING HOOK — Version 2 (No Job Posting / Base Signal)**

> "Hi [Name], my name is [Your name] from IGC. We work exclusively with independent IT MSPs in the UK — typically 5 to 50 people — building the outreach infrastructure that generates new managed service contract conversations, so the BD isn't entirely owner-dependent. I've been in the MSP space since 2017. Do you have 90 seconds?"

*Wait. If yes, continue immediately:*

> "So the reason I'm calling [Company name] specifically — you're a [X]-year-old independent MSP. Based on everything I know about MSPs at your stage, the typical situation is the owner is the only consistent BD engine. Engineers are delivering, not prospecting. LinkedIn gets used but there's no sequence behind it. New contracts come in through referrals, which is great until it isn't. Does any of that sound familiar?"

---

**TRANSITION TO DISCOVERY**

*When they confirm pain or say "yeah, that's pretty accurate":*

> "Good — that's exactly what we solve, but I want to make sure it's the right fit before I explain what we do. Can I ask you a few quick questions?"

*Wait for yes. Then move to discovery.*

---

**DISCOVERY QUESTIONS — In This Order**

Ask these in sequence. Do not pepper them all at once. One question, listen fully, then next.

**Q1 — Current BD engine:**
> "How are you winning new managed service contracts at the moment — is it mainly referrals, or do you have any proactive outreach running at all?"

*What you're hearing for: If purely referrals, owner-led BD is confirmed — that's the exact pain. If there's some outreach, understand what it looks like, who's running it, and how consistent it is.*

**Q2 — Current contract pipeline:**
> "How's the new business pipeline looking at the moment — are you signing new contracts consistently, or is that the bit you're actively working to fix?"

*What you're hearing for: Genuine pipeline scarcity vs. BD confidence. If they say "it's fine" — probe further before accepting it. Ask "how are contracts coming in at the moment — referrals, repeat business, or proactive outreach?" Referral-only is a vulnerability they may not have named yet.*

**Q3 — What they've tried:**
> "Have you tried anything on the BD side to generate more new contract conversations — Google Ads, SEO, LinkedIn, a marketing agency, cold calling, anything like that?"

*What you're hearing for: Tried and failed (best scenario — they know the gap, they've spent money, they're frustrated). Never tried (sceptical — need to earn credibility before pitching). Currently running something (go to script E — "We Already Have Something").*

**Q4 — Current pain specificity:**
> "When you think about new business right now — the biggest bottleneck: is it finding the right SMBs to approach, actually doing the outreach consistently, or converting the conversations you are having into signed contracts?"

*What you're hearing for: This tells you which part of the system resonates most. "Doing it consistently" = they know what to do but can't execute (owner bandwidth). "Converting conversations" = their pitch or their offer needs work. "Finding the right ones" = they need targeting and audience strategy. Note this for the pitch emphasis.*

**Q5 — Awareness of cost:**
> "Have you looked at bringing anyone in to help with BD — a dedicated hire, a sales agency, or a tool?"

*What you're hearing for: Budget awareness. If they've priced Robin Robins, MSP Marketing Edge, Ulistic, or a BD hire, they already know what MSP BD support costs. If they haven't, you'll frame the price differently.*

**Q6 — Decision dynamic:**
> "When it comes to a decision like investing in BD infrastructure — is that purely your call, or is there a co-director or business partner you'd bring in?"

*What you're hearing for: Solo decision or shared. If shared, never pitch without the decision-maker present. Book the discovery call with both.*

**Q7 — Urgency:**
> "What's driving the timing on this for you — is there a particular pressure point making new business more urgent right now?"

*What you're hearing for: Near-term drivers — an engineer they've just hired and need to keep billable, a client they just lost, a slow quarter, a competitor winning contracts in their patch, a downturn in MRR. Urgency = faster close.*

---

**THE 30-SECOND PITCH** (After Discovery Context is Established)

Do not pitch before Q3. You need to know what they've tried to make the pitch land.

*Transition:*
> "Okay — based on what you've told me, let me explain exactly what we do and you can tell me whether it makes sense."

*Pitch:*
> "We build a client acquisition system in 30 days — LinkedIn outreach sequences targeting the MDs and Finance Directors at SMBs in your patch who are either on break-fix, unhappy with their current MSP, or growing fast enough to need proper IT infrastructure. CRM pipeline, automated follow-up — all running on your LinkedIn account, all assets owned by you permanently. The sequences position you as the IT infrastructure partner for growing businesses in your area.

> The guarantee: 5 qualified prospect conversations in 30 days or we keep running the system at no charge until you hit that number.

> The investment is £3,500 to build it. After that, £1,750 a month if you want us to continue operating and optimising it — that's entirely optional. One new managed service contract covers the build fee. A single 30-seat business at £1,500/month MRR is £18,000/year ARR. The build pays for itself in the first contract the system generates."

*Pause. Let them respond.*

---

**GUARANTEE PRESENTATION** (If They Ask for More Detail)

> "The guarantee is specific. We guarantee 5 qualified prospect conversations within 30 days of launch. 'Qualified' means a two-way exchange with an SMB decision-maker — an MD, Finance Director, or Operations Director — who has confirmed a current or near-future IT need and agreed to continue the conversation. Not someone who said 'sounds interesting' and disappeared, not a cold reply, not 'not right now' — a real conversation with a genuine prospect. If we hit day 30 and you're at 3 or 4, we continue running the system — sequences live, audience refreshing — at no charge until you hit 5. We've been deliberate about the number: at conservative LinkedIn benchmarks, 5 is achievable. We've made it a guarantee because we're confident in the system."

---

**CLOSE TO NEXT STEP — Book the Discovery Call**

> "What I'd suggest is a 20-minute call — no pitch deck, no obligation. I ask you 6 or 7 questions about your MSP, your current BD situation, and what you're trying to build. At the end, you'll know exactly what the system would look like for [Company name] and whether it makes sense. If it doesn't fit, I'll tell you. Can you do 20 minutes this week or early next?"

*If they agree:* Confirm time, timezone (UK — confirm GMT/BST), give them a Calendly link or book directly. Confirm their email address for the calendar invite.

---

**HANDLING: "Can you send me some information first?"**

This is almost always a polite deferral, not genuine interest in reading. Do not accept it at face value.

> "Of course — I can send you something. I want to make sure I send you what's actually relevant to your situation rather than a generic overview. Based on what you've just told me — [one specific thing they said] — can I ask: is the interest there and you just want to have something in hand before we talk? Or is this more of a 'let me think about whether this is the right time'?"

*If they confirm genuine interest:*
> "Perfect. I'll send you a 2-page overview — it covers the system, the guarantee, the pricing, and a case study in the MSP space. Then let's book the 20 minutes for [day] — even if it's just to go through questions after you've read it. Does [specific day/time] work?"

*By booking the call, the email becomes a pre-read, not a substitute for the call.*

*If they're using it as a soft no:* Go to Script D (Timing Objection).

### B. LIVE ANSWER — SCEPTICAL / GUARDED

**Use when:** They answer with a guarded tone, a "what's this about?" curtness, or clear wariness about a sales call.

**Signs you're in this scenario:**
- Flat, clipped answer: "Yeah?"
- Immediate slight impatience: "Go ahead then."
- Suspicion in voice: "Who is this?"
- One-word answers in first 10 seconds

---

**THE OPENING — Do Not Change Your Opening Hook**

Run your standard opening hook (Version 1 or 2) at full pace. Do not adjust your tone to sound more apologetic — this signals hesitation and confirms their suspicion that this is a time-wasting call.

*After your opening hook, if they respond with short or guarded answers:*

---

**LOWERING THEIR GUARD — The "Not Here to Sell You Anything Today" Pivot**

> "I want to be upfront — I'm not going to try and sell you anything on this call. I'm calling 30 MSP owners this week; I've got 8–10 who are clearly in the right situation, and I need to work out if [Company name] is one of them. The way I do that is ask a couple of questions. If it's not a fit, I'll say so and get off the phone. Does that sound fair?"

*Wait. Most people will say yes or give a more open response here.*

---

**EARNING 60 MORE SECONDS**

If they're still short:
> "One question. How are you generating new managed service contracts at the moment, outside of referrals? Thirty seconds of your answer tells me whether this is worth 5 more minutes."

This works because:
- It's one question, not a pitch
- It names the vulnerability (outside of referrals) without accusation
- It implies the call ends quickly if the answer rules it out
- Almost every MSP owner in this niche will pause before answering, because the real answer is "mostly referrals"

---

**TRANSITION BACK TO DISCOVERY**

If they engage and give a real answer:
> "That makes sense. And is that working for where you want to take the business — or is there a gap you're actively trying to close?"

From here, if they open up, you're back in Script A territory. Move to Discovery Q1.

---

**IF THEY REMAIN CLOSED AFTER THE PIVOT**

Don't force it. A mildly interested person is not a good prospect.
> "I appreciate your time — I can hear this isn't a great moment. Can I ask one thing before I let you go: is it the timing today, or is the BD question just not a priority at the moment?"

If timing: "Understood. I'll try you [day of week] — would that be better?" Get a specific day.

If not a priority: "Got it. I'll leave it there. Good luck with the [specific thing you researched — e.g., new engineer, cyber launch]. Take care." Log in HubSpot as "Not Now — Low Priority." Set a 90-day follow-up task.

---

### C. LIVE ANSWER — PRICE OBJECTION ON A COLD CALL

**Use when:** They ask "how much does it cost?" or "what are your prices?" before you've completed discovery — typically after your opening pitch of who you are but before you've asked a single discovery question.

---

**THE RULE: Redirect First, Reveal Only If They Push**

Revealing price before discovery arms them with an objection before you've established value. You need their problem to be real and named before your price makes sense.

---

**REDIRECT SCRIPT**

> "I'll tell you exactly — I'm not going to be evasive about it. But genuinely the price changes based on what we find out in the next 3 minutes. It's one figure if you're starting from scratch, slightly different if you've already got a CRM running. Can I ask you two quick questions first so the number I give you is actually relevant to your situation?"

*If they agree:* Move to Q2 and Q4 of discovery (contract pipeline and what they've tried). Once you have that context, reveal price in the framing below.

---

**PRICE REVEAL SCRIPT** (After Discovery or If They Push Immediately)

> "So the investment is £3,500 to build the system — that's the 30-day sprint. After that, £1,750 a month if you want us to operate and optimise it, though that's genuinely optional. You own everything we build.

> The number that matters more: the £3,500 build fee — one new managed service contract at typical MSP rates covers it. A 30-seat business at £1,500/month MRR is £18,000/year ARR. That's the ROI case — and it tends to happen within the first 60 days for MSPs that run the system properly."

*Then immediately redirect:*
> "The question isn't really whether £3,500 is expensive — it's whether the system generates one managed contract in the first 60 days. And that's what I'd want to explore with you on a proper 20-minute call. Does Tuesday work?"

---

**IF THEY SAY "THAT'S TOO EXPENSIVE" BEFORE DISCOVERY**

Do not defend the price. Instead:
> "That's a fair reaction without knowing more. Can I ask: what are you currently spending on BD — whether that's time, paid tools, a marketing agency, or a fractional resource?"

If they say nothing: "Okay — and what's the typical MRR on a new managed contract for you? [£X]. So one new contract from the system covers the build in the first month's billing. If that math works, the price argument changes. Does it work?"

If it still lands cold, go to Script D (Timing Objection).

---

### D. LIVE ANSWER — TIMING OBJECTION

**Common forms:**
- "Not now, we're in a busy period"
- "Call me next quarter"
- "We're reviewing this in [month]"
- "I'm in the middle of a few things"
- "Not the right time"

---

**DIAGNOSING THE OBJECTION IN 10 SECONDS**

There are two types of "not now":

**Type 1 — Genuine timing** (a real reason they can't engage this week or month): They name a specific blocker — just onboarded a big client, mid-way through a major cyber project, half their team is at an event, about to go on holiday. This is real. Respect it and get a specific callback date.

**Type 2 — Soft no** (the call is going nowhere and they're being polite): Vague deferral. Can't name a specific reason. "Just not the right time" with no detail. This is a soft no dressed as a timing objection.

*Diagnostic question:*
> "Of course — can I ask what's making this month difficult? I want to make sure I'm calling back at a time when it's actually useful."

A real reason (holiday, project onboarding, Cyber Essentials audit, mid-migration) = Type 1. Vague or evasive = Type 2.

---

**TYPE 1 — GENUINE TIMING RESPONSE**

> "That makes complete sense. When would be the right time — are we talking end of [month], or are you thinking more like [next quarter]?"

*Force specificity:*
> "If I were to put a note in my diary to call you on [specific date], would that land at a better moment?"

When they agree on a date:
> "Perfect. I'll call you [day, date] at around [time]. I'll send you a quick email in the meantime so you've got context before we speak — what's the best email?"

Log in HubSpot: Call Back Agreed. Set task for exact date.

---

**TYPE 2 — SOFT NO RESPONSE**

Don't push hard. Get one more piece of information before you end the call.
> "Understood. Before I let you go — honest question: is the BD challenge something you're actively looking to solve this year, or is it more in the 'someday' category at the moment?"

If "someday": "Okay — I'll put you on a 6-month touch. I'll drop you an email with a couple of case studies in case the timing changes. Is that alright?"

If "this year, just not now": "Alright. I'll call you at the end of [month]. One last thing — when new contracts are slow, what's usually causing it: not enough prospect conversations, or conversations that aren't converting to signed contracts?" (This question often reopens the conversation even in a Type 2 scenario.)

---

**HOW TO END THE CALL SO THEY REMEMBER THE CONVERSATION**

For any timing objection, end with a pattern interrupt. Something that makes the call memorable:
> "Last thing before I go — you mentioned [specific thing they said, e.g., 'we're getting referrals but they're slowing down']. That's the exact situation I built this system for. When you revisit this, that's where we start. I'll follow up in [timeframe]."

This works because:
- You're repeating their own words back to them
- You're framing their problem as the starting point for IGC's solution
- You're giving the follow-up call a specific anchor, not a generic "just checking in"

### E. LIVE ANSWER — "WE ALREADY HAVE SOMETHING" / "WE USE X"

**Common forms:**
- "We're already using Robin Robins / MSP Marketing Edge / Ulistic"
- "We've got someone doing our marketing"
- "We've got a BD hire starting next month"
- "We've got a marketing agency running LinkedIn for us"
- "We have an in-house account manager handling that"

---

**DO NOT IMMEDIATELY CHALLENGE WHAT THEY HAVE**

Your first move is to understand what "X" actually means. Most MSP owners using a generic MSP marketing service are getting content, brochures, and a newsletter — not actual prospect conversations. Let them show you the gap.

---

**DISCOVERY QUESTIONS TO UNDERSTAND WHAT "X" IS**

> "That's great to know. Can I ask — what does that look like in practice? Are they running LinkedIn outreach, or is it more content marketing and SEO?"

*Listen for:* Monthly retainer, ongoing dependency, no ownership of the assets, content-only output with no outbound component.

> "And in terms of results — how's the new contract pipeline looking from that activity specifically?"

*Listen for:* Vague answers, pivoting to "it's early days", "we're still getting it set up", "we get some MQLs but nothing's closed yet", or a long pause.

> "And if you were to stop using them tomorrow — do you keep the sequences, the CRM data, the content? Or does it all sit with them?"

*This is the ownership angle question. The answer is almost always "it stays with them."*

---

**POSITIONING IGC DIFFERENTLY — THE OWNERSHIP ANGLE PIVOT**

> "That's the difference between what we do and what you've described. With Robin Robins or MSP Marketing Edge or a typical marketing agency, you're renting the pipeline — the moment you stop paying, the outreach stops and the assets go with them. We do the opposite: we build it in 30 days, we hand it over, and you own it permanently. You can run it yourself or have us operate it, but it's yours either way.

> The question isn't 'do you have something' — it's 'do you own what you're paying for.'"

*Pause. Let that land.*

> "I'm not saying switch. What I'm saying is it might be worth a 20-minute conversation to understand the difference — particularly because we guarantee 5 qualified prospect conversations in 30 days or we run it for free. Does that sound like a conversation worth having?"

---

**IF THEY HAVE AN IN-HOUSE BD PERSON**

> "That makes sense. And are they doing cold outbound to brand-new prospects, or more account management and upsell on the existing book?"

*Most "in-house BD people" at 5–50 person MSPs are actually account managers doing upsell and renewal, not dedicated BD infrastructure builders running cold outreach.*

> "The reason I ask is most in-house BD hires at MSPs your size end up doing account management and upsell rather than cold outreach for new managed service contracts — which is a different skill and a different system. Is that new contract pipeline coming in consistently, or is there still variability?"

---

### F. LIVE ANSWER — HARD NO (Won't Engage At All)

**Signs you're here:**
- "Not interested" within 5 seconds of your opening
- "We don't take sales calls"
- "Take me off your list"
- "This isn't something we'd ever consider"

---

**DO NOT ARGUE — CLOSE GRACEFULLY**

> "Absolutely, I'll respect that. I apologise for the interruption."

*Pause for 1 second — this disarms the instinct to hang up immediately.*

---

**THE "LAST THING" QUESTION**

*Used selectively — only if their "no" sounded like a reflex rather than an informed decision:*

> "One last thing before I go — and this is genuinely the last thing: is it that you're happy with your current BD situation, or is it more that you get a lot of calls like this and it's just not the right moment?"

*If the latter:* "That's fair. I'll send you a two-paragraph email so you can see what we do in writing — easier than a call. If it's not for you, just delete it. Is that okay?"

*If confirmed happy with BD:* "Understood — I'll note that and won't follow up. Good luck with [specific thing you know about them — e.g., the new engineer, the cyber practice]. Take care."

---

**WHAT NEVER TO DO ON A HARD NO**

- Do not defend the pitch
- Do not ask "are you sure?"
- Do not give a lengthy explanation of why they're wrong
- Do not promise to call back in a week

A hard no with grace leaves the relationship intact. They may refer someone. They may call you when their circumstances change.

---

**WHAT TO LOG IN HUBSPOT AFTER A HARD NO**

Stage: Disqualified
Sub-status: Hard No — Refused Engagement
Notes: "[Date] — Hard no on cold call. Tone: [described the tone]. Reason given: [if any]."
Task: Do NOT create a follow-up call task. Create a 6-month recontact review task — they may have different circumstances.

### G. VOICEMAIL SCRIPT

---

**SHOULD YOU LEAVE A VOICEMAIL?**

**Rules:**

- **First attempt:** No voicemail. Hang up silently. Call again on a different day/time.
- **Second attempt (same number, different day):** No voicemail. Try a different time window.
- **Third attempt:** Leave Version A voicemail (below). Send a LinkedIn message the same day.
- **Fourth attempt, if no response:** Leave Version B voicemail (below). Send an email the same day.
- **Fifth+ attempt:** Stop calling. Move to email/LinkedIn sequence only. Tag in HubSpot as "Voicemail - No Response." Do not call again for 30 days.

**Rationale:** Multiple voicemails with no callback = they screen unknown numbers. Switching channels after attempt 3 respects their preference and keeps the relationship intact.

---

**DURATION RULE**

Under 25 seconds. Time it. Every word above 25 seconds loses them.

---

**VERSION A — THIRD ATTEMPT (First Voicemail)**

> "Hi [Name], [Your name] calling from IGC. I work with independent IT MSPs — we build client acquisition infrastructure. I've called a couple of times — won't keep trying without reaching you. I'll drop you a LinkedIn message today. If you're curious, my number is [number]. No pressure either way."

**Duration target: 18–22 seconds.**

**What NOT to say:**
- "I was just calling to check in" (no purpose)
- "I'd love to connect" (transactional cliché)
- "This is really important" (pressure, condescending)
- Any price or feature mention (too much too fast)

**What to do immediately after leaving this voicemail:** Send LinkedIn connection request with a 300-character note that mirrors the voicemail: "Hi [Name] — [Your name] from IGC. Just left you a voicemail. We build client acquisition systems for independent IT MSPs. Worth a look if new contract pipeline is a priority for you. Happy to connect."

---

**VERSION B — FOURTH ATTEMPT (Second Voicemail, Different Angle)**

> "Hi [Name], [Your name] from IGC — last message, I promise. I'll be honest: I've tried you a few times because [Company name] fits the exact profile of MSP we work with. I've also sent a LinkedIn message. If the new business pipeline question is live for you, I'm at [number] or [email]. If the timing's wrong, no problem — I'll check back in a few months."

**Duration target: 20–25 seconds.**

**What to do immediately after leaving this voicemail:** Send a short email (see Section 6 — Post-Call Protocol for the exact email template).

---

**SHOULD YOU REFERENCE AN EMAIL OR LINKEDIN MESSAGE IN THE VOICEMAIL?**

Yes — always. It sets an expectation that something is coming. They'll look for it. The voicemail becomes a heads-up, not a standalone ask. This increases the likelihood they read the email or accept the LinkedIn connection.

### H. GATEKEEPER / RECEPTIONIST

**Context:** Most 5–50 person independent IT MSPs do not have a traditional receptionist. More common is a service desk analyst, office manager, or junior engineer who picks up the main line, or an EA at MSPs at the upper end of the headcount range. True gatekeeping is less common in this ICP than in larger firms, but it happens.

---

**THE DIRECT ASK — For Small MSP Settings**

Most small MSP main lines are informal — you're often talking to a service desk analyst between tickets. Use directness:
> "Hi — could I speak to [Name] please? It's [Your name] calling."

No company name, no explanation upfront. Just a direct ask. In small MSPs, this usually works. They'll put you through or say "[Name] is in a client meeting" — at which point ask for a direct number or mobile.

---

**THE SOFT APPROACH — For Formal Receptionists**

> "Hi — [Your name] calling for [Name]. I'm in IT sector advisory — it's a quick call, shouldn't take more than a couple of minutes. Is [Name] available?"

Note: "IT sector advisory" is accurate, not deceptive. You are advising IT MSPs.

---

**IF THE GATEKEEPER ASKS "WHAT'S IT ABOUT?"**

Never say "it's about business development services." That's a guaranteed transfer to voicemail.

Say:
> "It's a brief commercial conversation — I work specifically with IT MSPs. [Name] would know whether it's relevant."

This is honest and puts the decision back on the MD without triggering a hard filter.

---

**IF THE GATEKEEPER SAYS "HE/SHE DOESN'T TAKE UNSOLICITED CALLS"**

> "Understood. Could you let [Name] know [Your name] from IGC called? I'll follow up by email. Could I get their direct email address, or should I go through the main contact email on the website?"

Always exit gracefully and always get something — even just confirmation of a better contact route.

---

**WHAT NEVER TO SAY TO A GATEKEEPER**

- "They're expecting my call" — never lie
- "It's a personal matter" — this is unethical and will destroy trust if they ever find out
- "It's urgent" — creates false urgency and damages credibility on transfer

## SECTION 3 — DISCOVERY FRAMEWORK

### Discovery Objective

By the end of discovery, you should know:
1. Whether this is a qualified prospect (worth booking a 20-minute call)
2. Which part of the IGC offer resonates most (build only, or build + operate)
3. What their urgency level is
4. Who else is involved in the decision

If you cannot answer all four, discovery is incomplete.

---

### The 5–7 Questions in Order

| # | Question | What You're Qualifying |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | "How are you winning new managed service contracts at the moment — mainly referrals, or do you have any proactive outreach running?" | Size + BD dependency on owner |
| Q2 | "How's the new business pipeline — are you signing new contracts consistently, or is that the bit you're working to fix?" | Pain severity + urgency |
| Q3 | "Have you tried anything on the BD side — Google Ads, SEO, LinkedIn, a marketing agency, cold calling?" | Prior spend + sophistication + objection profile |
| Q4 | "When you think about new business now — biggest bottleneck: finding the right SMBs, doing the outreach consistently, or converting conversations to contracts?" | System gap identification — informs which IGC deliverable to emphasise |
| Q5 | "Have you looked at bringing anyone in to help — a dedicated hire, a sales agency, or a tool?" | Budget awareness + competitive context |
| Q6 | "When it comes to investing in BD infrastructure — is that your call alone, or is there a co-director or partner?" | Decision dynamics — never pitch to the wrong person |
| Q7 | "What's driving the timing on this — is there a pressure point making new business more urgent?" | Urgency scoring — Tier A/B/C for follow-up priority |

---

### What a Qualified Prospect Looks Like

A prospect is qualified when they meet ALL of the following:

- **Size:** 5–50 employees confirmed (sweetspot 8–25 — or close enough that Q6 has the right decision-maker)
- **Pain confirmed:** They acknowledge new contract pipeline is a gap, a vulnerability, or an active problem — even indirectly ("we're mostly referrals, which is fine but unpredictable")
- **Decision-maker:** The person you're speaking to can make or significantly influence the buying decision (MD / Founder / Owner)
- **No hard blocker:** No active long-term contract with a competitor delivering results, no company wind-down, no hiring freeze making the whole premise irrelevant
- **Budget plausibility:** Managed service contract ARR is consistent with MSP market rates (£10,000+/year per contract) — one new contract will cover the build

**A qualified prospect does NOT need to have expressed interest in buying.** They need to have confirmed enough of the pain for the discovery call to be worth both parties' time.

---

### Hard Disqualification Criteria — Do Not Book a Discovery Call

Stop the call (politely) and log as Disqualified if:

| Criterion | Why |
|---|---|
| Headcount below 5 (confirmed) | Survival mode — budget is too tight and owner bandwidth for onboarding a system isn't there. |
| Headcount above 50 (confirmed) | Almost certainly has or is about to hire a dedicated BD person. IGC is not the right fit. |
| Not a genuine MSP (IT staffing, IT recruitment, pure break-fix with no managed contracts) | IGC's system is built for managed contract sales — different sales model entirely. |
| Pure break-fix or transactional project work only | Target prospects, value props, and close motion are all different. Not a fit. |
| Actively in liquidation / wind-down | This is public information — check Companies House if uncertain before calling. |
| Currently locked in a 12-month contract with Robin Robins, MSP Marketing Edge, or comparable — and it's delivering | They can't buy. Flag for 90-day follow-up when contract nears end. |
| Decision-maker has left / company acquired | Update Apollo and HubSpot. Identify new contact if appropriate. |
| The prospect explicitly says they have a dedicated sales/BD hire who is performing | This is a rare hard stop. A performing BD hire means the pain is genuinely solved. Move on. |

---

### Handling a Prospect Who Wants to Skip Discovery and Just Hear the Pitch

**This happens.** They say: "Just tell me what you do" or "What's your pitch?" before you've asked anything.

Do not refuse. Give them the 30-second pitch first, then redirect to discovery:

> "Happy to — [30-second pitch as written in Section A]. That's the model. Now — the reason I don't lead with that is it means nothing until I know what's actually going on in your business. Two questions: how are you winning new managed contracts right now, and is that working consistently enough to keep the engineers billable?"

You've satisfied their request, then immediately pulled them back into qualifying. Most will engage with the questions at this point.

## SECTION 4 — CALL PACING AND RULES

### How Many Attempts Per Number

| Attempt | Action | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Dial. No answer / straight to voicemail — hang up silently. | No voicemail. |
| 2 | Dial again at least 24 hours later, different time window. No answer — hang up. | No voicemail. |
| 3 | Dial. If no answer — leave Version A voicemail. Send LinkedIn message same day. | |
| 4 | Dial 3–5 days later. If no answer — leave Version B voicemail. Send email same day. | |
| 5+ | Stop calling this number. Move to email/LinkedIn channel only. | Flag in HubSpot: "Voicemail — No Response." 30-day cooling-off before next call. |

**If you reach them on attempt 2 or later:** Do not reference previous attempts unless they bring it up. Start fresh with your opening hook.

---

### Time Between Attempts

| Attempt Gap | Minimum Interval | Maximum Interval |
|---|---|---|
| Attempt 1 → 2 | 24 hours | 72 hours |
| Attempt 2 → 3 | 48 hours | 5 business days |
| Attempt 3 → 4 | 3 business days | 7 business days |
| After voicemail left | 5 business days minimum | — |

**Never call the same number twice in one day.** It reads as aggressive and will cause the number to be blocked.

---

### Time of Day Rules (UK, GMT/BST)

| Window | Assessment | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 07:30–09:00 | Early. MD may be in before the service desk spins up. Lower guard. | Selective — only for confirmed mobile numbers of Tier 1 prospects |
| 09:00–10:00 | Monday operational triage window — morning ticket backlog. Low answer rate. | Avoid on Mondays. Use Tue–Thu. |
| **10:00–12:00** | **Best window.** Day is running, urgency not yet peak, MD is at a desk. | **Primary calling block.** |
| 12:00–13:30 | Lunch. Inconsistent. | Acceptable if you can't avoid it. |
| 13:30–15:30 | Decent. Often in-office hours for smaller MSPs. | Secondary calling block. |
| **16:00–17:30** | **Second best window.** Day wrapping up, ticket queue calming, more relaxed. | **Primary calling block.** |
| 17:30–18:30 | Declining. Often on commute or closing down. | Tier 1 only — some MSP owners work late. |
| After 18:30 | Do not call. | — |

**Best days: Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday.**

Monday: Lower engagement — operational catch-up and weekend ticket backlog in progress.
Friday afternoon: Mentally checked out. Decisions don't happen. Poor investment of call time.

**UK bank holidays:** Do not call. Build this into your dial block calendar.
- January: New Year's Day
- April: Good Friday, Easter Monday
- May: Early May Bank Holiday, Spring Bank Holiday
- August: Summer Bank Holiday
- December: Christmas Day, Boxing Day

---

### If They Ask "How Did You Get This Number?"

This happens. Have the answer ready. It is honest and concise.

> "It came through a data provider we use — Apollo. Your number was listed as a business direct dial for [Company name]. If you'd rather I didn't call this number, I'll remove it right now — is there a better way to reach you?"

**The second sentence is important.** Offering to remove the number and asking for a better contact route simultaneously:
- Demonstrates good faith
- De-escalates any irritation
- Gives them agency, which reduces the adversarial dynamic
- Sometimes results in them giving you a preferred mobile or email

**If they escalate (GDPR concern):** "Understood — I'll remove your number from our records immediately and won't contact you again via this channel. I apologise for the interruption." Then log in HubSpot: "Requested removal — data contact. Do not call." Send yourself an internal task to remove their number from your Apollo/Cognism list within 24 hours.

---

### When to Stop Calling and Switch to Another Channel

Switch to email + LinkedIn only when:

1. Five call attempts with no answer (including 2 voicemails left)
2. They explicitly asked to be contacted by email only
3. They've asked you not to call this number (give them your email and LinkedIn instead)
4. Their voicemail box is full (often signals a number is no longer monitored)
5. The number appears to be disconnected or always diverts to a generic voicemail without their name

**Switching channel is not giving up.** Email + LinkedIn sequence running in parallel with calling is correct. Some prospects respond to LinkedIn who would never pick up a call.

## SECTION 5 — PRICE NEGOTIATION RULES

These are hard rules. They are not guidelines. Do not improvise around them.

---

### RULE 1: When Do You Reveal the Price?

**Standard answer: After discovery, before the close.**

Price comes after you've heard their situation. Not at the top of the call. Not in the first three minutes. You reveal price when:
- You've confirmed they're qualified (Q1–Q3 answered)
- You've given the 30-second pitch
- The conversation has established that the problem is real

**Exception: If they ask directly before discovery, use the Script C redirect. If they push after the redirect, reveal the full price with the ROI framing. Do not hide the price — it damages trust. Redirect it once, then give it cleanly.**

---

### RULE 2: Is the £3,500 Build Fee Negotiable?

**No. The build fee is fixed.**

The build fee anchors the entire engagement. Discounting it:
- Signals that the price was not credible to begin with
- Removes the strongest objection-handling tool you have ("one new contract covers it")
- Sets a precedent for negotiating everything that follows

The only exception is the promotional scenario defined in Rule 6.

**If they push back on the build fee:** "The build is fixed — I don't move on it because the number already reflects what a 30-day build engagement costs. What I'd point to instead is the ROI argument: a single managed contract from conversations the system generates covers the build in full. If you're signing SMB contracts at anywhere near market MRR, this pays for itself before the retainer clock even starts."

---

### RULE 3: Is the £1,750/Month Retainer Negotiable?

**Rarely, and only under specific conditions.**

The retainer is the ongoing business. It is not a throwaway concession.

Conditions under which a retainer adjustment may be considered:
- They commit to a minimum 6-month retainer contract upfront (not month-to-month) — in this case, £1,500/month is the floor
- They are a genuine referral from an existing IGC client (referred-client pricing: see Rule 6)

**What never happens:** Discounting the retainer on a cold call to close the build. If they want a cheaper retainer, they sign a longer contract.

---

### RULE 4: What Is the Absolute Floor?

| Component | Standard | Absolute Floor | Condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Build fee | £3,500 | £3,500 | Non-negotiable |
| Monthly retainer | £1,750/month | £1,500/month | 6-month minimum contract only |
| Bundle (build + 6 months retainer) | £14,000 | £12,500 | Pre-payment in full (both build + 6 months paid upfront) |

Below these numbers, the engagement economics do not work. Do not accept below the floor under any circumstances. If a prospect pushes below the floor, you have the wrong prospect — not the wrong price.

---

### RULE 5: When Do You Offer a "Special"?

**A promotional offer is triggered by ONE of the following:**

1. **Callback within 48 hours:** They end the discovery call interested but not decided. They call back within 48 hours to book. (This is the most common trigger.)
2. **End-of-month close:** You are in the final week of the calendar month, you have capacity available, and the prospect is clearly qualified but sitting on the fence. The month-end framing is legitimate.
3. **Referral situation:** They were referred by a current or past IGC client. A referral fee or discount is appropriate in this context only.

**Not a trigger:**
- They asked for a discount on the cold call
- They said "that seems expensive"
- They've expressed price sensitivity without a serious conversation having taken place
- You're feeling pressure to close and want to use a discount as momentum

Discounting without a trigger devalues the offer and trains the prospect to negotiate as a default.

---

### RULE 6: What Does the "Special" Look Like?

**The promotional offer has three possible structures. Use one at a time — never stack.**

**Option A — Reduced Build (Referral/48h callback only):**
Build fee: £2,750 (saving £750). Retainer unchanged at £1,750/month.
Frame: "Because [you were referred by X / you came back to us quickly] — I can move the build to £2,750 for this engagement."

**Option B — First Month Retainer Free (End-of-month close only):**
Build fee: £3,500 (unchanged). Month 1 retainer: £0. Month 2 onwards: £1,750.
Frame: "We have a build slot open this month. If you're ready to go — I can include the first month of retainer at no charge. That makes the 30-day build the only upfront cost."

**Option C — Extended Guarantee (Sceptical qualified prospect, not price-related):**
Build fee: £3,500. Retainer: £1,750/month. Guarantee extended from 30 to 45 days.
Use this when price is not the blocker but risk is. "I'm confident enough in the system that for this engagement, I'll extend the guarantee to 45 days. If you haven't hit 5 qualified conversations by day 45, we keep running it at no charge."

---

### RULE 7: What Never Gets Discounted?

- The build fee (except Option A trigger)
- The retainer below £1,500/month under any circumstances
- The number in the guarantee (5 conversations — lowering this devalues the outcome signal)
- The 30-day build timeline (rushing it or extending it to "save money" both break the system)
- Asset ownership — this is non-negotiable in every engagement regardless of pricing

## SECTION 6 — POST-CALL PROTOCOL

**This section covers what happens in the 30 minutes following every call.** Speed matters. Same-day follow-up from a cold call is the difference between a conversation that converts and one that dies in inbox noise.

---

### OUTCOME A: Discovery Call Booked

**Within 15 minutes of hanging up:**

**1. HubSpot Update:**
- Stage: Call Booked
- `igc_outreach_stage`: booked
- Add note: "[Date] — Cold call. Spoke [X] minutes. Confirmed pain: [one sentence on what they said]. Discovery call booked: [date/time]. Decision-maker status: [sole / shared — note if partner/co-director involved]."
- Create task: "Send discovery call confirmation email" — due today
- Create task: "Prepare prospect brief" — due 1 day before discovery call

**2. Calendar Invite:**
Send immediately from your calendar (Google/Outlook). Include:
- Subject: "IGC — 20-Minute Discovery Call | [Company name] + IGC"
- Date, time, duration (20 minutes)
- Video link (Zoom or Google Meet)
- One-line agenda: "We'll cover your current BD situation, how the IGC system works, and whether it makes sense for [Company name]."

**3. Confirmation Email:**

Send within 30 minutes of hanging up. Use this exact template:

---

Subject: Confirmed — Discovery Call [Date] at [Time]

Hi [Name],

Confirmed for [Day, Date] at [Time] (UK time).

[Video link]

20 minutes. I'll cover how the client acquisition system works for a [size]-person IT MSP, what the build looks like in practice, and the guarantee mechanics. You'll have everything you need to make a call by the end.

One thing to look at before we speak if you have 3 minutes: [link to IGC website or a specific page that's most relevant — e.g., the guarantee section if they asked about it].

Talk soon,
[Your name]
IGC

---

**What NOT to include in the confirmation email:**
- Attachments (opens a parallel evaluation track before the call)
- Pricing (they should hear it in context, not in an email)
- Lengthy company background
- "Let me know if you have any questions before the call" (vague open-ended)

---

**4. Discovery Call Prep (1 Day Before the Call):**

Prepare a one-page brief covering:
- Company name, founding year, headcount, services they deliver from their website (managed IT, cyber, cloud, Microsoft, vCIO)
- Key signal that triggered the call (job posting, LinkedIn activity, new service launch, award/certification)
- Pain points confirmed on the cold call (verbatim if you noted them)
- What they said they've tried before
- The one thing you want to understand better on the call
- Decision-maker status

Have this open during the call.

### DAILY CALL SESSION PROTOCOL

**Before your dial block:**
- Pull today's list from HubSpot (Tier 1 first, then Tier 2 and 3)
- Confirm all numbers are verified (mobile or direct dial only)
- Check UK bank holidays
- Set time window: 10:00–12:00 or 16:00–17:30

**During your dial block:**
- Take notes in real time — verbatim on key phrases (especially pain points in their own words)
- Do not check email during a dial block
- Log each attempt in HubSpot before moving to the next dial

**After your dial block:**
- All HubSpot updates complete
- All follow-up emails/LinkedIn messages sent
- All tasks created
- Review: how many connects, how many discoveries booked, what objections came up that weren't in this document (add them)
